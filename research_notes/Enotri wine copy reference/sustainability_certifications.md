# Wine Sustainability & Organic Certifications: Official Wording, Canadian Claim Rules, and Verification of Enotri's Sustainability Page

Research date: 15 October 2026. Quotes are short and attributed, with exact URLs. This is not legal advice. Items marked "confirm" should go to a Canadian advertising/regulatory lawyer, the certifier, or the producer.

Method note: agriculture.gouv.fr (the French Ministry of Agriculture) could not be fetched in this session. HVE findings therefore come from French chambers of agriculture, the trade press and Conseil d'État coverage, not from a ministry quote. Some findings came from search-engine summaries rather than full-page reads; they are marked "(secondary)".

## 1. EU organic (Reg. (EU) 2018/848), the EU organic logo ("Euro-leaf"), and organic wine rules

### Takeaway
EU organic certification guarantees that grapes and winemaking comply with Regulation (EU) 2018/848 (in force since 1 Jan 2022), verified by an approved control body at least once a year. It **restricts** synthetic pesticides and fertilisers rather than banning all "synthetic chemicals", and it caps sulphites below conventional limits. The EU logo is **mandatory only on pre-packaged organic food produced in the EU**, optional on imports, and always appears with the control body's code.

### Cited Findings
**Administration and legal basis**
- The new EU organic legislation "applies from 1 January 2022" — [European Commission, Organics at a glance](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organics-glance_en)
- Reg. 2018/848 explicitly "revoga o Regulamento (CE) nº 834/2007 do Conselho" (repeals Council Regulation (EC) No 834/2007). Its list of authorised products and substances is in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/1165 — [Kiwa Portugal (PT-BIO-03)](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)
- Commission definition: "Organic farming is an agricultural method that aims to produce food using natural substances and processes." — [EC, Organics at a glance](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organics-glance_en)
- Control: each EU country appoints control bodies or authorities, and "All operators are checked at least once a year to make sure that they are continuing to follow the rules." — [EC, Organics at a glance](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organics-glance_en)

**What it guarantees and what it does not**
- Synthetic inputs are *restricted*, not banned outright: "A utilização de fertilizantes, pesticidas ou medicamentos veterinários de síntese química é restringida" ("the use of chemically synthesised fertilisers, pesticides or veterinary medicines is restricted") — [Kiwa Sativa, an EU-accredited certifier](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)
- GMOs are excluded: "Os produtos biológicos são livres de organismos geneticamente modificados (OGM) e de radiações ionizantes." — [Kiwa Sativa](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)
- Organic growers may use sulphur and copper against vine disease (secondary; merchant tasting coverage) — [Country & Town House, Sustainable Wine guide](https://www.countryandtownhouse.com/food-and-drink/sustainable-wine-guide/)

**The EU organic logo**
- Official description: "twelve white stars shaped in the form of a leaf on a green background" — [EC, The organic logo](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-logo_en)
- "The organic logo can only be used on products that have been certified as organic by an authorised control agency or body." — [EC, The organic logo](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-logo_en)
- "The logo can only be used on products when they contain at least 95% organic ingredients" (with further strict conditions for the remaining 5%) — [EC, The organic logo](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-logo_en)
- Mandatory for "all pre-packaged EU food products, produced and sold as organic within the EU". Optional for "imported products where the product conforms to the EU rules on the import of organic goods" — [EC, The organic logo](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-logo_en)
- "Next to the EU organic logo, a code number of the control body must be displayed", plus where the raw materials were farmed (EU / non-EU agriculture) — [EC, The organic logo](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-logo_en)
- The logo cannot be used on products "in 'conversion'" — [EC, The organic logo](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-logo_en)

**Organic wine rules (SO2 and winemaking)**
- The "organic wine" category dates from the 2012 rules (Implementing Reg. (EU) 203/2012, applying from August 2012), which banned sorbic acid and desulphurisation and required lower sulphites (secondary) — [Jancis Robinson, "Organic wine defined"](https://jancisrobinson.com/articles/organic-wine-defined); [Farming News](https://farming.co.uk/news/new-eu-rules-for-%E2%80%98organic-wine%E2%80%99-agreed)
- Current limits under 2018/848, from a certifier's summary dated 4 May 2022. Red wine with residual sugar below 2 g/l: "maximum 100mg/l". White and rosé below 2 g/l: "maximum 150mg/l". At 2 g/l residual sugar or more, the conventional maximum "must be reduced by at least 30mg/l". A temporary derogation is possible in exceptional weather years — [Ceres, Brief Info Production of Organic Wine Reg (EU) 2018/848](https://ceres-cert.co.za/files/3-2-737_EN_Brief%20Info%20Organic%20Wine%20Production%20EU%20Reg%202018%20848_Inf.pdf)
- Comparison: "An organic red is limited to 100 mg/L of total SO2, against 150 mg/L for a conventional red." — [Château d'Arsac](https://chateau-arsac.com/en/sulfites-in-wine/)
- Prohibited in organic winemaking: partial concentration through cooling, physical removal of SO2, electrodialysis, cation exchangers and partial dealcoholisation. Restricted: filtration pores not below 0.2 µm, heat treatment not above 75 °C — [Ceres brief](https://ceres-cert.co.za/files/3-2-737_EN_Brief%20Info%20Organic%20Wine%20Production%20EU%20Reg%202018%20848_Inf.pdf)
- About 50 additives and processing aids remain authorised, with organic sourcing "if available". Examples: yeasts, tartaric acid, bentonite, gelatin, egg albumin, casein, gum arabic, enzymes — [Ceres brief](https://ceres-cert.co.za/files/3-2-737_EN_Brief%20Info%20Organic%20Wine%20Production%20EU%20Reg%202018%20848_Inf.pdf)

**How credible sources describe it**
- In Portugal, the label code is explained like this: "Junto ao logo encontra-se o código da entidade responsável pelo controlo" ("next to the logo is the code of the body responsible for control") — [Sabor Bio](https://www.saborbio.com/pt/why-organic/)

### Inferences
- The 95% rule matters little for wine, which is essentially 100% grapes. What matters is that the grapes are organic **and** the cellar follows the organic winemaking annex.
- "Strict limits on additives and sulphites" is defensible. "No synthetic chemicals" is not: synthetic inputs are restricted to an authorised list, and many conventional additives remain permitted.
- Imported organic wines in Canada will often carry the Euro-leaf, but under EU law it is optional on exports. Copy should not say the logo is "mandatory" without qualifying that.

### Gaps
- Did not read the consolidated EUR-Lex text of Reg. 2018/848 Annex II Part VI, so post-2022 amendments are unconfirmed. In particular, whether partial dealcoholisation of organic wine was later authorised after the EU's 2024–2025 wine-package changes. Confirm with EUR-Lex or the certifier before stating a full list of prohibited practices.
- The conventional white/rosé limit (200 mg/L) was not confirmed from a primary source in this session.

## 2. France: AB logo, HVE (levels 1–3 and 2022–2025 revisions/criticism), Ecocert, Terra Vitis, Vignerons Engagés

### Takeaway
AB is France's optional state-owned organic logo for EU-organic products. **Ecocert is a certification body, not a standard.** HVE is the top tier (level 3) of France's state environmental certification for farms. It is results-based, but it is **not organic** and has been criticised as weak. A greenwashing challenge was rejected by the Conseil d'État on 18 Feb 2025. Terra Vitis and Vignerons Engagés are private schemes recognised only at HVE **level 2**.

### Cited Findings
**AB (Agriculture Biologique)**
- The AB logo is the property of the French Ministry of Agriculture. Agence Bio has managed it for communication purposes since 1 October 2007. It is optional, and the EU logo has been mandatory on pre-packaged organic food since 1 July 2010 (secondary, older sources) — [Réussir, "L'Agence Bio gestionnaire du logo AB"](https://www.reussir.fr/fruits-legumes/lagence-bio-gestionnaire-du-logo-ab); [Assemblée nationale QE (13th legislature)](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/13/questions/QANR5L13QE88633.pdf)
- AB indicates at least 95% organic ingredients, with certification by a state-approved third-party body (secondary) — [Chambre d'agriculture Martinique, 2015](https://martinique.chambre-agriculture.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/National/FAL_commun/publications/Martinique/Se_convertir_a_l_agriculture_Biologique_2015.pdf)

**Ecocert (certification body)**
- Biodyvin "decided to entrust [inspections] to an independent body, ECOCERT SAS FRANCE", which inspects vineyards and vinification and reports to the union. This illustrates Ecocert's role as an inspector or certifier against someone else's standard — [Biodyvin, Approval](https://www.biodyvin.com/en/biodyvin-approval.html)

**HVE: structure**
- HVE is level 3 of France's environmental certification of farms. Level 1 is regulatory compliance and level 2 is an obligation of means. Only level 3 grants the HVE certification and logo, assessed on results indicators across four themes: biodiversity, plant-protection strategy, fertilisation management and water-resource management. Certification lasts 3 years and is issued by an accredited certification body (secondary) — [Chambre d'agriculture Creuse, Nouveau référentiel HVE](https://creuse.chambre-agriculture.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/Nouvelle-Aquitaine/098_Inst-Creuse/PDF/grandes_cultures/Nouveau_referentiel_HVE.pdf); [Réussir, nouveau cahier des charges](https://www.reussir.fr/certification-hve-tout-savoir-sur-le-nouveau-cahier-des-charges)
- The legal basis is article L. 611-6 of the Code rural, as cited in the Conseil d'État reasoning (secondary) — [Pleinchamp, CE decision](https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/le-referentiel-hve-conforme-et-pas-trompeur-selon-le-conseil-d-etat)

**HVE: 2022–2025 revisions and criticism (dates)**
- A new reference was set by Decree 2022-1447 and the arrêté of 18 Nov 2022 (Journal officiel, 22 Nov 2022), applicable from **1 Jan 2023**. It removed "voie B", the accounting route based on inputs as a share of turnover, leaving voie A (indicators) as the only route. HVE became an access route to the 2023–2027 CAP eco-scheme (secondary) — [Pleinchamp, "2023 un nouveau départ pour la HVE"](https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/2023-un-nouveau-depart-pour-la-hve); [Chambre d'agriculture Tarn-et-Garonne](https://agri82.chambre-agriculture.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/Occitanie/075_Inst-Tarn-et-Garonne/8-Archives/Archives_2023/actualites_2023/2023-01_Changement_de_referentiel_v3.pdf)
- A note from the French Biodiversity Office (OFB) found the requirements "too weak" (secondary) — [Réussir, critical evaluation report](https://www.reussir.fr/hve-publication-dun-rapport-devaluation-tres-critique-apres-sa-revision)
- In January 2023, seven organisations (FNAB, UFC-Que Choisir, Synabio, Générations Futures, Bio Consom'acteurs, Réseau Environnement Santé, Agir pour l'Environnement) asked the Conseil d'État to annul HVE as misleading greenwashing — [Réseau Environnement Santé press release](https://www.reseau-environnement-sante.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/230123_CPinterasso-Recours_HVE_VF.pdf); [UFC-Que Choisir](https://www.quechoisir.org/action-ufc-que-choisir-label-hve-il-trompe-le-consommateur-la-justice-doit-le-condamner-n105322)
- **18 Feb 2025:** the Conseil d'État rejected the challenge, finding the reference neither unlawful nor misleading. The public rapporteur noted it "peut être insuffisamment exigeant" (may be insufficiently demanding) on points such as CMR2 plant-protection products and fertilisation. The ministry targets 50,000 HVE farms by 2030 (secondary) — [Pleinchamp](https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/le-referentiel-hve-conforme-et-pas-trompeur-selon-le-conseil-d-etat); [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Le-Conseil-d-Etat-valide-la-certification-HVE); [UFC-Que Choisir reaction](https://www.quechoisir.org/action-ufc-que-choisir-les-organisations-requerantes-contre-le-label-hve-prennent-acte-du-rejet-de-leur-recours-par-le-conseil-d-etat-et-restent-mobilisees-face-aux-insuffisances-du-referentiel-n149460/)

**Terra Vitis and Vignerons Engagés**
- Terra Vitis was created in 1998 and is recognised by the ministry, but only at HVE **level 2** (since 2012). The ministry said HVE equivalence was "not considered feasible". Terra Vitis growers can obtain HVE through combined audits; Terra Vitis Alsace members reached HVE 3 by collective audit in 2018 (parliamentary answers, 2019–2021) — [Assemblée nationale QE 19837](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE19837.pdf); [QE 21390](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE21390.pdf)
- Vignerons Engagés is described as an environmental certification recognised by the Ministry, equivalent to HVE level 2, with four axes including support for local territory and heritage (retailer brochure, secondary) — [Nicolas, Brochure labels](https://nicolas.com/medias/sys_master/root/h23/h48/9855659933726/Brochure-labels/Brochure-labels.pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- "France's highest level of environmental certification for farms" is literally accurate **within the state's environmental-certification scheme**, since HVE is its level 3. But the general impression that HVE outranks organic is misleading: organic is a separate, more prescriptive EU regime.
- HVE does not ban synthetic pesticides. The rapporteur's CMR2 comment shows such products remain usable. Copy should not imply pesticide-free farming.
- "Certified organic by Ecocert" is the correct construction, and it also matches Canada's CFIA rule (Section 7) that "certified organic" must be tied to the certification body's name.

### Gaps
- No verbatim quote from agriculture.gouv.fr (blocked in this session). Get the ministry's own HVE wording before quoting it on the site.
- Exact 2023 indicator thresholds were not verified.
- Ecocert France's EU code (widely given as FR-BIO-01) was not verified from a primary source here. Read it off Château de la Grave's label or certificate.
- Vignerons Engagés' ISO 26000 basis and audit details were not verified.
- The AB-specific wine rule found in a French source ("vin issu de raisins de l'agriculture biologique") appears to predate the 2012 "organic wine" rules and is probably outdated. Not relied on.

## 3. Portugal: what "PT-BIO-03" is

### Takeaway
PT-BIO-03 is **not a standard**. It is the EU code of a Portuguese **organic control/certification body, Kiwa Sativa** (formerly SATIVA Desenvolvimento Rural, Lda.), which certifies to EU Reg. (EU) 2018/848. Calling it "a certification standard under EU Regulation (EC) No 834/2007" is wrong twice: it is the wrong type of thing, and 834/2007 was repealed in 2022.

### Cited Findings
- "O código europeu de organismo de certificação para a Agricultura biológica da Kiwa Portugal (Kiwa Sativa) é o PT-BIO-03." ("The European organic certification-body code of Kiwa Portugal (Kiwa Sativa) is PT-BIO-03.") — [Kiwa Portugal](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)
- Kiwa Sativa is accredited by the Instituto Português de Acreditação for organic production "de acordo com o Regulamento (UE) 2018/848" — [Kiwa Portugal](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)
- The national list shows "PT-BIO 03 - Kiwa Sativa – Unipessoal, Lda." — [Observatório Nacional da Produção Biológica](https://producaobiologica.pt/index.php/producao-biologica/controlo). An older DGADR list shows "PT-BIO 03. SATIVA, Desenvolvimento Rural, Lda." — [DGADR](https://www.dgadr.gov.pt/images/docs/val/bio/Biologica/OC_contactos_MPB.pdf)
- Kiwa's certificates can be checked in the EU TRACES organic operator database, and Kiwa partners with Demeter International for biodynamic certification — [Kiwa Portugal](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)
- Reg. 2018/848 repealed 834/2007, and the new framework has applied since 1 Jan 2022 — [Kiwa Portugal](https://www.kiwa.com/pt/pt/servicos/certificacao/pt-bio-03-certificacao-agricultura-biologica/)

### Inferences
- Correct framing: "certified organic under EU Regulation (EU) 2018/848 by Kiwa Sativa (control body PT-BIO-03)."

### Gaps
- Not confirmed that Quinta da Palmirinha is certified by PT-BIO-03. Check its current certificate (TRACES) or bottle label.

## 4. Biodynamic: Demeter International, Biodyvin, and legal status

### Takeaway
"Biodynamic" is defined by **private, trademarked standards**: Demeter worldwide and Biodyvin for wine in France and Europe. No government regulation defining it was found. Both standards build on organic certification. Biodyvin members must be AB-certified, and Biodyvin inspections are carried out by Ecocert.

### Cited Findings
- "Demeter certification is a guarantee for consumers that products come from biodynamic agriculture." — [Demeter International, Certification](https://demeter.net/certification/)
- "Demeter certification is a regular inspection and review process according to the International Demeter Biodynamic Standard". It is carried out by member organisations in listed countries; South Africa is not among them. Elsewhere, "the Federation has its own certification organisation (ICO)" — [Demeter International](https://demeter.net/certification/)
- Demeter USA requires a farm to "first meet the requirements of NOP organic as a base" (secondary search excerpt) — [Demeter USA](https://demeter-usa.org/demeter-biodynamic-certification/)
- Biodyvin (Syndicat International des Vignerons en Culture Bio-Dynamique) set its vine and vinification specifications in 1998. Its inspections are entrusted to "ECOCERT SAS FRANCE", and "After four years of conversion to biodynamic agriculture, the SIVCBD issues official BIODYVIN approval" — [Biodyvin](https://www.biodyvin.com/en/biodyvin-approval.html)
- "All of the SIVCBD's member properties are therefore certified AB (Organic Agriculture) by an inspection body". Its winemaking aim: "nothing added, nothing taken out, nothing changed." — [Biodyvin](https://www.biodyvin.com/en/biodyvin-approval.html)
- Biodyvin's founding year is given as 1995 in one source and 2002 in another (conflict) — [wein.plus glossary](https://glossary.wein.plus/biodyvin)
- Merchant framing of biodynamics: the aim is for the vineyard "to function as its own self-regulating ecosystem", using preparations and a lunar calendar (secondary) — [Country & Town House](https://www.countryandtownhouse.com/food-and-drink/sustainable-wine-guide/)
- Reyneke was certified biodynamic by Demeter International in 2008 (secondary) — [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/life/food-and-wine/article-south-african-winemaker-organic-farming)

### Inferences
- Since biodynamic has no legal definition in the EU or Canada (as far as this research found), the only defensible claim is "certified biodynamic by Demeter" or "Biodyvin-approved", naming the body.
- In Canada, "organic" claims on a biodynamic wine still depend on organic certification recognised under the Canada Organic Regime (Section 7). Demeter alone does not authorise an "organic" claim.

### Gaps
- Did not find an affirmative official statement that "biodynamic" has no legal definition. This is inferred from the absence of any regulation and the private trademark structure. Confirm with counsel if the page will say so.
- Reyneke's organic certifier and its CFIA-recognised status were not verified.

## 5. South Africa: IPW, SWSA, the Integrity & Sustainability seal, WIETA, Fairtrade

### Takeaway
The green Integrity & Sustainability seal certifies **origin, vintage and variety** (Wine of Origin) **plus IPW environmental compliance** for every farm, cellar and bottler involved. It does **not** certify ethics or labour. Since **1 Aug 2023** the issuing body is the **Wine Certification Authority (WCA)**, formerly the Wine and Spirit Board, with SAWIS as its agent. WIETA is a social and labour audit scheme with a "Fair Labour Practice" seal. Fairtrade requires certification across the whole trade chain, and logo use needs a licence.

### Cited Findings
**IPW (Integrated Production of Wine)**
- "Integrated Production of Wine (IPW) is the environmental sustainability scheme established by the South African wine industry in 1998." The 2000 vintage was the first certified — [IPW](https://www.ipw.co.za/)
- "Certification of IPW compliance falls under the jurisdiction of the Wine and Spirit Board (WSB), with a dedicated IPW office responsible for administering the scheme". Practices on farms and cellars "are evaluated and audited by the board" — [IPW](https://www.ipw.co.za/)
- IPW states it complies with the FIVS Global Wine Sector Environmental Sustainability Principles and the OIV guidelines for sustainable viti-viniculture — [IPW](https://www.ipw.co.za/)
- The IPW pass mark is 65%. Climate change and carbon footprint were added to the guidelines from 2011 — [WOSA, SWSA FAQs](https://www.wosa.co.za/swsa/en/FAQs/)
- IPW's site now hosts a document titled "EU Directive Greenwashing" — [IPW](https://www.ipw.co.za/)

**Integrity & Sustainability seal**
- "The original seal only certifies origin, vintage and varietal. The new seal also certifies sustainability." Producers choose which seal to use — [WOSA, SWSA FAQs](https://www.wosa.co.za/swsa/en/FAQs/)
- "To use the seal, 100% of the fruit must be IPW accredited. This includes each link in the production chain, from cellar to bottling plant." — [WOSA, SWSA FAQs](https://www.wosa.co.za/swsa/en/FAQs/)
- Ethics are separate: "Wines meeting ethical production standards can carry either a Fairtrade or Wine Industry Ethical Trade Association (WIETA) accreditation." — [WOSA, SWSA FAQs](https://www.wosa.co.za/swsa/en/FAQs/)
- The joint WO and IPW seal has been available "as from the 2010 harvest year". It is "a visual guarantee, not only for the integrity of origin and/or vintage year and/or cultivar, but also for sustainable production". IPW calls it "a world first" — [IPW](https://www.ipw.co.za/)
- "The Wine of Origin system traces the bottle of wine all the way back to the vineyard"; the seal links the vineyard to its growing practices. Seal check: sawis.co.za/sealsearch.php — [WOSA, SWSA Traceability](https://www.wosa.co.za/swsa/en/Traceability)
- **Renamed body (1 Aug 2023):** "the Wine and Spirit Board (Wyn en Spiritusraad) is now known as the Wine Certification Authority". Its functions (the Wine of Origin, IPW and Estate Brandy schemes) are unchanged. Redesigned seals with the new name and a **QR code** have been in operation since January 2025. Seals confirm the wine "has been verified by the WCA, through its agent SAWIS and third party auditor, Enviroscientific, from the vineyard to its final packaging". New site: wineauthority.org — [WCA letter to participants, 23 Jan 2025](https://www.sawis.co.za/cert/download/Letter_to_participants_of_the_schemes-_23_January_2025.pdf)
- Legal basis: the Liquor Products Amendment Act 8 of 2021 creates the WCA to replace the Wine and Spirit Board. Gazette numbers conflict between sources (49061 in the WCA letter vs 49064 on gov.za) — [gov.za, Liquor Products Amendment Act 8 of 2021](https://www.gov.za/documents/acts/liquor-products-amendment-act-8-2021-english-afrikaans-17-sep-2021)

**WIETA**
- WIETA is "a multi-stakeholder, non-profit voluntary organisation which actively promotes ethical trade in the wine industry value chain through training, technical assessment and audits". It was established in 2002 after a two-year ETI pilot, with supply-chain auditing introduced in 2010. It reported 1,362 active members in 2025 — [WIETA home](https://www.wieta.org.za/)
- "Regular independent and external third-party audits are conducted to assess and evaluate the auditee member organisation's adherence and compliance to the WIETA standard." Verification is by triangulation: documents, worker and management interviews, and site inspections — [WIETA, Who we are](https://wieta.org.za/who-are-we/)
- Scope listed by WIETA: "management systems, human and labour rights, working conditions, health and safety, work housing and community wellbeing". Environment is not listed — [WIETA, Who we are](https://wieta.org.za/who-are-we/)
- Code basis: the ETI Base Code and South African law. Principles cover no child labour, freely chosen employment, health and safety, freedom of association, non-excessive hours, a living wage, non-discrimination, and housing and tenure security (secondary) — [Durham University repository paper](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1528951); [BSI](https://bsigroup.com/en-ZA/industries-and-sectors/food-and-drink/wieta)
- WIETA's "Fair Labour certification Seal was introduced in May 2012". Every grower, cellar and bottler must pass traceability checks, a self-assessment, an audit and verified corrective actions, submitting corrective action plans "within a maximum of 4 months of audit date" — [WIETA, Fair Labour Practice Wine Seal](https://wieta.org.za/fair-labour-practice-wine-seal/)
- WIETA's own 2025 top risk areas: working hours, regular employment, housing and tenure security, chemical handling and storage — [WIETA home](https://www.wieta.org.za/)
- Criticism: two 2017 WIETA audits rated Groot Constantia "major noncompliance" — [Mail & Guardian, 2018](https://mg.co.za/article/2018-03-16-00-constantia-leaves-bitter-aftertaste)

**Fairtrade (Fairtrade International / FLOCERT)**
- "In Fairtrade, the entire trade chain (producers, exporters, importers, manufacturers, distributors and brand owners) must be Fairtrade certified." — [FLOCERT, Get certified](https://www.flocert.net/get-certified/)
- "Fairtrade certification by FLOCERT does not automatically grant the right to use the FAIRTRADE Mark on product packaging or labels." Mark use requires a licence from Fairtrade International or a national Fairtrade organisation — [FLOCERT](https://www.flocert.net/get-certified/)
- Wine grapes fall under Fairtrade standards (Hired Labour and small-producer organisations). Under the Fresh Fruit HL standard, "You pay the Premium within 60 days of purchase of unprocessed or processed wine grapes from producers." — [Fairtrade, Fresh Fruit standard (HL)](https://www.fairtrade.net/content/dam/fairtrade/fairtrade-international/standards/fresh-fruit/Fresh_Fruit_HL-EN.pdf)
- Fairtrade's own caveat: "In many commodities, the Fairtrade Standards, auditing and corrective measures are insufficient for reaching living wages." — [Fairtrade Risk Map, Living wage](https://riskmap.fairtrade.net/salient-issues/living-wage)
- A 2014 change replaced an earlier shared-equity requirement for South African wine with the Hired Labour Standard (secondary) — [GroundUp briefing paper](https://groundup.news/media/uploads/documents/briefing_paper_fairtrade_research_1.pdf)

### Inferences

## 7. Canada: "organic" claims for imported wine (Canada Organic Regime, SFCR Part 13, Canada–EU equivalency, logo, AGLC)

### Takeaway
Enotri may call an imported wine "organic" in Canadian labels **and advertising** only if it is certified under the Canada Organic Regime. For EU wines, that means EU-certified under the **Canada–EU equivalency arrangement**, which covers wine produced or finally processed **in the EU**. **South African organic wines are outside that arrangement.** They need certification to the Canadian Organic Standards by a CFIA-accredited body (confirm). "Certified organic" must name the certifier. AGLC requires a valid organic certificate and declaration form for every imported organic product.

### Cited Findings
**Scope and claims (CFIA, page modified 27 Apr 2026)**
- "Imported or interprovincially traded products making an organic claim must be certified under the Canada Organic Regime." — [CFIA, Organic claims on food labels](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)
- "Only products with organic content that is greater than or equal to 95% may be labelled or advertised as "organic" or bear the organic logo." Terms like "organically grown" count as organic claims — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)
- "All aspects of food labels and advertising contribute to the overall impression a food product makes." — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)
- The label must name the certification body. "Certified organic" claims "are considered misleading if they are used without being in connection to the name of the certification body" — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)
- "100% organic" is not permitted in Canada. "Made with organic ingredients" is not acceptable — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)
- **Canada Organic logo:** voluntary, only for products with at least 95% organic content certified under the Canada Organic Regime. Imported products bearing it must show "Product of [country]" or "Imported" near the logo, in English and French. Using it for information is allowed only if "the advertisement is not used to sell a food, feed or seed product". Misuse "could be an offence" under the SFCA — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)
- Imports from equivalency partners "may bear the Canada organic logo" if certified to the terms of the arrangement — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)

**Canada–EU equivalency (EUCOEA)**
- "This equivalence arrangement took effect in June 2011". Under it, "Agricultural products, including wine, are eligible to be imported to Canada as organic if produced and processed in the European Union" and certified by an EU-approved control body, with an operator certificate — [CFIA, EUCOEA overview](https://inspection.canada.ca/organic-products/equivalence-arrangements/eucoea-overview/eng/1328074578961/1328074648480)
- "Organic products that are grown, produced and packaged outside of the EU are not within the scope of the EUCOEA, even if they are certified to EU standards." — [CFIA, EUCOEA](https://inspection.canada.ca/organic-products/equivalence-arrangements/eucoea-overview/eng/1328074578961/1328074648480)
- "EU operators can use the Canada organic logo in accordance with the Part 13 of the SFCR." Both logos may appear — [CFIA, EUCOEA](https://inspection.canada.ca/organic-products/equivalence-arrangements/eucoea-overview/eng/1328074578961/1328074648480)
- The CFIA page (modified 11 Aug 2025) still cites the repealed EU Regs 834/2007 and 889/2008 — [CFIA, EUCOEA](https://inspection.canada.ca/organic-products/equivalence-arrangements/eucoea-overview/eng/1328074578961/1328074648480)
- Wine was not in the original 2011 scope. A revised CFIA letter of recognition effective 7 Apr 2016 covers EU products including wine (secondary) — [CFIA, Letters exchanged](https://inspection.canada.ca/organic-products/equivalence-arrangements/european-union-letters-exchanged/eng/1328075123452/1328075318193); [Livingston International](https://www.livingstonintl.com/canadian-organic-industry-to-benefit-from-expanded-canadaeu-organic-equivalency-arrangement/)

**Alberta (AGLC)**
- AGLC lists Canada's equivalency arrangements: US, EU, Switzerland, Costa Rica, Japan, Taiwan, UK, Mexico and South Korea. **South Africa is not listed** — [AGLC, Organic Liquor Products](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-agencies-and-suppliers/organic-liquor-products)

Since **26 May 2025**, the CFIA requires a valid English or French organic certificate for organic shipments, or the shipment is rejected. Certificates are normally valid one year. Each certificate needs a declaration form sent to organic-certificates@aglc.ca, and agencies are responsible — [AGLC](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-agencies-and-suppliers/organic-liquor-products)

This conflicts with Alberta's own page, which says organic certification has been required for in-province sales since 1 Apr 2019 under the Supporting Alberta's Local Food Sector Act (secondary) — [Alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-organic-products-regulations.aspx). Not material for imports, which fall under federal rules anyway.

## 8. Canada: Competition Act rules on environmental and "green" claims (Bill C-59, the 2025 guidance, Bill C-15)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026:
- Any environmental claim must not be false or misleading in its **general impression**.
- Claims about a **product's** environmental benefits must rest on **adequate and proper testing done before the claim**.
- Claims about a **business's** environmental benefits need **adequate and proper substantiation**. Since **26 Mar 2026** (Bill C-15) this no longer has to follow an "internationally recognized methodology".
- The Bureau's six principles (5 Jun 2025 guidance) remain the practical playbook. The Bureau says it will update the guidance for C-15.

### Cited Findings
**Timeline**
- **June 2024:** Bill C-59 added s. 74.01(1)(b.1) (product environmental benefits, which need adequate and proper testing) and (b.2) (business or business-activity environmental benefits, which need adequate and proper substantiation per an internationally recognized methodology) — [Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/updates/further-amendments-environmental-claims-provisions-competition-act/)
- **5 Jun 2025:** the Bureau published its final guidelines, "Environmental claims and the Competition Act" — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/06/canadian-competition-bureau-releases-much-anticipated-guidance-on-greenwashing-provisions); [Canada.ca news](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2025/06/competition-bureau-issues-final-guidelines-regarding-environmental-claims.html)
- **20 Jun 2025:** private parties could start applying for leave to bring deceptive-marketing cases to the Tribunal — [Competition Bureau guidance, FAQ 9](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/publications/environmental-claims-and-competition-act)
- **26 Mar 2026:** Bill C-15 (Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1) received Royal Assent. "These changes removed the requirement for environmental claims to be supported by an internationally recognized methodology. We will update our guidance" — [Competition Bureau, Environmental claims and greenwashing](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/environmental-claims-and-greenwashing)
- C-15 also bars private parties from getting Tribunal leave over another party's **business-activity** environmental claims. Private actions over **product** environmental claims remain possible — [Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/updates/further-amendments-environmental-claims-provisions-competition-act/); [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/federal-government-narrows-scope-of-the-competition-acts-anti-greenwashing-provisions-as-bill-c-15-receives-royal-assent/)
- The substantiation duty for business claims "has not been repealed". The testing requirement for product claims "has not been amended or repealed" — [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/federal-government-narrows-scope-of-the-competition-acts-anti-greenwashing-provisions-as-bill-c-15-receives-royal-assent/)

**Current rules (Bureau landing page, modified 1 Jun 2026)**
- The provisions "prohibit claims about the environmental benefits of a product that are not based on adequate and proper testing" and claims about a business "that are not based on adequate and proper substantiation" — [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/environmental-claims-and-greenwashing)
- A "representation" includes online ads, social media messages, promotional emails and endorsements. "General impression" is assessed alongside literal meaning — [Bureau guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/publications/environmental-claims-and-competition-act)

**The six principles (5 Jun 2025 guidance)**
1. Truthful, not false or misleading. "do not rely on a disclaimer or fine print to cure an otherwise misleading environmental claim."
2. Product environmental benefit and performance claims must be adequately and properly tested.
3. Comparative claims must be specific about what is compared.
4. Avoid exaggeration: "While even small benefits to the environment can be positive, they should not be marketed as big ones."
5. Be clear and specific, not vague. "eco-friendly" may imply benefit "throughout its entire life cycle". "when in doubt, spell it out."
6. Future claims need substantiation and a clear plan.
— all from the [Bureau guidance](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/publications/environmental-claims-and-competition-act)

**FAQ points relevant to a small importer**
- "Foreign businesses marketing in Canada must comply". There is a due-diligence defence that avoids penalties, restitution and corrective notices, though not a cease order. Substantiation need not be published. "The Act does not expressly require third party verification", but it "may improve the credibility of claims". For small businesses, "testing is required regardless of the size of the business making the claim." — [Bureau guidance, FAQs 7, 12, 14, 15, 27](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/publications/environmental-claims-and-competition-act)

### Inferences
- **Highest risk on Enotri's page:** "positive social and environmental impact" (Claim 9) and "Guided by Responsibility / Verified, not merely claimed". These invite product- or business-level environmental-benefit readings that must be tested or substantiated in advance.
- **Lowest-risk pattern:** state third-party certifications by name and scope, per estate ("certified organic by Ecocert"), and keep a certificate file.
- Third-party certifications help but do not cure an overstated general impression. A certification only substantiates what it actually certifies.

- Administrative monetary penalty levels were not sourced in this session. Have counsel confirm the current maximums.
- The Bureau's post-C-15 updated guidance had not been published as of the pages read (1 Jun 2026 landing page).
- The EU Empowering Consumers Directive and its timing (referenced by IPW) were not researched. It is relevant only if Enotri's copy reaches EU audiences.

## 9. How credible importers and merchants write sustainability copy without greenwashing

### Takeaway
Respected merchants label farming **estate by estate** and separate **"certified" from "practising"**. They describe what a scheme *is* rather than what it *achieves*, and keep company-level green claims to things they have measured (for example, carbon-neutral status).

### Cited Findings
- Kermit Lynch lets shoppers filter by "organic certified; organic practicing; biodynamic certified; biodynamic practicing; sustainable, traditional; Haute Valeur Environmentale certified; and Lutte Raisonnée" (secondary) — [The Drinks Business, Dec 2025](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/12/kermit-lynch-opens-second-retail-space/)

- Berry Bros. & Rudd's eco page frames its commitments as pursuing high-quality wine and promoting the best environmental standards "where consistent with that pursuit", and working with like-minded suppliers (paraphrase from search summary; verify verbatim) — [BBR, Eco information](https://www.bbr.com/about/eco-information)
- Liberty Wines' company-level green claims are measured ones: carbon-neutral status in 2014 and carbon-neutral-plus in 2021 (secondary) — [National Restaurant Awards](https://www.nationalrestaurantawards.co.uk/stories/wine-a-sustainable-future.html); [Caravan & Co](https://caravanandco.com/blogs/journal/caravan-meets-liberty-wines)
- Les Caves de Pyrene frames itself through values (terroir, indigenous grapes, organic viticulture, minimal intervention) rather than certifications (secondary) — [Les Caves de Pyrene](https://www.lescaves.co.uk/lescaves-home)
- WOSA's SWSA pages split topics (Biodiversity, Integrity, Sustainability, Traceability, Climate Change, Ethical Trading, Organic and Biodynamic) and answer with checkable specifics: the 65% pass mark, 100% IPW fruit, and which seal covers what — [WOSA, SWSA FAQs](https://www.wosa.co.za/swsa/en/FAQs/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended pattern for Enotri:**
  - A per-estate "How it's farmed" line that names the scheme and certifier, for example "Certified organic, Ecocert" or "WIETA Fair Labour Practice; IPW".
  - A short glossary of what each scheme covers and doesn't.
  - "Practising" or "farmed organically, not certified" wherever certification is absent.
  - Reserve sensory, terroir language for the wine, not for claims that the farming causes a quality outcome.
- Boutique tone fits this approach: warm grower stories plus precise, named credentials. That keeps the "Verified, not merely claimed" promise literally true.
