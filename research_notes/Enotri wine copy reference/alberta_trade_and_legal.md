## 1. How Alberta's privatized supply system works for a wine agency

### Takeaway
AGLC is the legal importer of record and the only legal seller to the trade; the agency is the "consignee" that owns the stock in the AGLC/CLS warehouse until a licensee buys it. Licensees (restaurants, bars, hotels = Class A; liquor stores = Class D) buy either from the CLS warehouse (full cases, six-digit AGLC product code, a 25-case minimum per order across all products, payment to AGLC, order/delivery charges to CLS) or from a liquor store; private individuals can only buy through a licensed retail liquor store, which can bring in any registered product on request.

### Cited Findings

**AGLC's role and the agency's legal position**
- "All liquor products in Alberta must be sold through AGLC. AGLC is the purchaser (importer of record) while the agency is the consignee." (LA 3.1.1) — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- "A liquor agency is prohibited from selling liquor other than through AGLC." (LA 2.1.7); "An agency may not accept deposits for liquor aside from futures" (LA 2.1.8). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- "Agencies are prohibited from selling liquor under any circumstances and may only accept deposits for Futures" (LA 3.17.2); an agency is prohibited from "receiving deposits from non-licensees" (LA 3.21.2 b). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- AGLC's public page: "AGLC is the legal importer of record for liquor in Alberta. Manufacturers and suppliers sell liquor products to businesses (licensees) through AGLC." and "All liquor products sold in Alberta are registered with AGLC." — [AGLC, Liquor products in Alberta](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-products-alberta)
- Definitions: "licensee" means the holder of "a Class A – D liquor licence"; "liquor agency" means "a corporation or individual who is in the business of representing a liquor supplier in the sale of the supplier's liquor" (LA 1.2.1 i, k). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Connect Logistics Services (CLS) — name and role are current**
- AGLC (2026 page): "Connect Logistics Services Inc. (CLS) warehouses and distributes spirits, wine, coolers and imported beer in Alberta under contract with AGLC. Currently CLS manages the main warehouse in St. Albert as well as additional storage spaces in Edmonton." CLS ships "to licensees in more than 320 Alberta communities." The page links a "Connect Logistics 2026 Agent & Licensee Rates" PDF. — [AGLC, Warehouse and distribution](https://aglc.ca/liquor/warehouse-and-distribution)
- The Handbooks call it the "Central AGLC Authorized Warehouse" (owned by AGLC) and name the operator "Connect Logistics Services Inc." (LA 1.2.1 b; LH 6.1.1 a, dated 9 June 2026). — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)
- CLS's website is **connect-logistics.com** (not connectlogistics.ca); its customer service line is 1-800-265-6784. — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)
- "Connect Logistics Services does not own the product in our distribution center. We are a 3rd party distributor of liquor products. The product belongs to the Agency or Supplier until it is sold to a licensed buyer." — [LiquorConnect FAQ](https://www.liquorconnect.com/Home/Faq)
- "Each Supplier or Agency determines which of its products it will offer for sale in Alberta and how much inventory is available" — [LiquorConnect FAQ](https://www.liquorconnect.com/Home/Faq)

**Liquor Connect — still the name of the ordering/search site**
- LH 6.2.2: "Orders may also be placed through the CLS website: www.liquorconnect.com. To register for online services, call CLS at 1-800-265-6784." — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)
- CLS: "Internet Ordering is available on LiquorConnect" (licensee portal at portal.liquorconnect.com). — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)
- Public side of LiquorConnect.com shows which stores have recently received a product from CLS (e.g. "Received in the last 3 months if the wholesale price is < $29.99 per unit"; 12 months for $30–$89.99 or slow sellers; 24 months for $90+), and tells shoppers "it's important that you call ahead to the Retailer to confirm product availability." — [LiquorConnect FAQ](https://www.liquorconnect.com/Home/Faq)

**Who can buy from the warehouse, and how**
- LH 6.1.1: "AGLC is the sole importer of liquor into Alberta. All liquor offered for sale in a licensed premises must be purchased by the licensee from one of the following AGLC-approved sources: a) the Central AGLC authorized warehouse (i.e., Connect Logistics Services Inc.); b) a liquor supplier or liquor agency authorized to warehouse and distribute products (e.g., domestic brewery); or c) a Class D retailer authorized to sell to licensees". — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)
- LH 6.2.5–6.2.6: information required to place any order: "name of licensed retail outlet; AGLC licence number; six-digit product code for each item ordered (see the Liquor Wholesale Price list); and quantities required (full case)." "Licensees may purchase liquor from an authorized Class D retailer." — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)
- LH 6.2.1: for "order days, minimum order quantities, product pick-up and delivery, contact CLS" (8:00–4:30 Mon–Fri; 1-800-265-6784 or 780-418-6500 Edmonton). — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)

**Minimums**
- CLS order minimum: "Why do I have to order a minimum of 25 cases? All freight rates are based upon weight… Lowering the case minimum would also bring most licensees directly to Connect Logistics Services to order as opposed to buying their products from a retail outlet." New businesses that struggle with "the 25 case minimum every week" are told to order larger quantities every other week. — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)
- Licensees may pool orders: "Two or more licensees may consolidate their orders (i.e., place their orders together) to satisfy minimum order requirements" — one licence number, one bill of lading, placing licensee pays in full (LH 6.1.3). — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)
- Agency side: "Agencies are not permitted to impose a minimum order quantity per product per licensee" (LA 3.6.5); "Agencies may impose a maximum order quantity per product per licensee" (LA 3.6.7). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- CLS will not do single-bottle distribution unless all suppliers/agencies "unanimously agreed" and paid the extra cost. — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)

**Pricing**
- "The price of liquor must be the same, at any one time, for all licensees regardless of the quantity purchased." (LA 3.8.1) Price changes are weekly: quotes due "4:00 p.m. Wednesday for implementation on the Friday of the following week" (LA 3.8.2). The single-unit price must be the same in any case configuration or mixed case (LA 3.8.4). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- Retail: "Stores may set their own retail prices. Consumer response and market forces will influence pricing" (CLS quoting AGLC retail guidelines); LH 6.2.7: "Pricing information is available on AGLC's website". — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees); [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf)

**Payment and delivery**
- "All payments are made directly to the AGLC. Connect Logistics Services does not handle payments." Licensees can be held to a "Bank Guarantee Letter" limit; payment questions go to AGLC Finance (780) 447-8673. — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)
- Order and delivery charges are paid to CLS: "Connect is a third party warehouser and distributor so transportation costs are not built in to the wholesale cost of the product." Short-shipped or damaged cases go to CLS Customer Service (cs@connect-logistics.com); complaints about faulty product go to AGLC (780) 447-8832 "or the Supplier or Agency of that product." — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)
- Out-of-stock: "The Supplier, Agency or manufacturer are responsible for ensuring that their products are in the warehouse"; to find out when a product returns, "Contact the Supplier or Agency directly." — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)
- Agencies are paid by AGLC weekly "for all product sold the previous week" (LA 3.7.1), net of warehouse charges (LA 3.13.3). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Product registration and codes (the "CSPC" question)**
- LA 3.2.1: "National Canadian Standard Product Codes (CSPCs) are no longer available. If there is an existing National CSPC for the product, AGLC will register the product in Alberta using that number. AGLC will continue to assign its own internal Product Code to new products." LA 3.2.2 says agencies "must use the Product Code" with AGLC. — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- LH 6.2.5 calls it the "six-digit product code"; LiquorConnect search uses the "SKU #" (e.g. "756811"). — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf); [LiquorConnect FAQ](https://www.liquorconnect.com/Home/Faq)
- Trade press still uses "CSPC" informally (e.g. readers told to search "the CSPC code at Liquorconnect.com"). — [Culinaire magazine, Sept 2020 (Issuu)](https://issuu.com/culinairemagazine/docs/culinaire_9.3_september_2020/38) (secondary; seen via search result only)
- Registration: all products registered through the online Liquor Agency Portal (LA 3.1.7); "It will take AGLC up to three weeks to process a product registration" (LA 3.1 note); supplier signs a "Letter of Authorization" and agency a "Letter of Understanding" (LA 3.1.2–3.1.3); "Each product must not be represented by more than one agency at the same time" (LA 3.1.4); containers must first be registered with the Beverage Container Management Board (LA 3.12.2); products must meet Canadian labelling rules (LA 3.1.15); for a product to be formally recognized as organic, "the word organic is to be included in the name/description" (LA 3.1.9 c). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- After registration "AGLC will provide instructions… to access the Liquor Agency Portal so you can register new products and manage your own pricing." — [AGLC, To become a liquor agency](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-agencies-and-suppliers/become-liquor-agency)

**"Allocation" is a defined AGLC term (trade only)**
- "Allocation of a product means a product which has been specifically held for select customers upon request of an agency." Limited to 1,000 cases per Product Code per calendar year (not available for products selling over 2,000 cases); "Only those customers who have been designated by the agency, through the allocation process, will be allowed to purchase the product"; set up via CLS at Clients@Connect-Logistics.com (LA 3.9.1–3.9.7). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- On the public LiquorConnect site, "Restricted Allocated products will be visible to the consumer but will have a masked SKU #. The consumer will be prompted to contact the agency for more information on the product." — [LiquorConnect FAQ](https://www.liquorconnect.com/Home/Faq)
- "A supplier or agency cannot be set up to provide an exclusive product to a licensee or group of licensees through the allocation process or any other means." (LA 2.5.5) — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**How private individuals actually buy an agency's wine**
- "Can I order products to ship TO MY home? No, you can have a liquor store order products for you. Connect Logistics does not distribute to consumers." — [LiquorConnect FAQ](https://www.liquorconnect.com/Home/Faq)
- LA 3.26.5 lists consumer options: search LiquorConnect.com ("Thousands of liquor products are registered for sale in Alberta, but may not be stocked by all retail liquor stores"); "Speak with staff or management at retail liquor stores. Products which are not on the shelves may be brought in at customer request"; stores "work with registered liquor agencies" to bring in unavailable products; "Individuals may also contact agencies directly"; stores that choose to may take orders online and deliver to adults. — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- LA 3.26.1 b: an adult may cause liquor to be brought into Alberta by contacting a Class D retail liquor store "or… a registered liquor agency" and requesting the product and quantity; "There are no minimum or maximum quantity requirements." Product so imported "may be picked up from, or delivered by, the retail liquor store licensee to the adult who ordered it" (LA 3.26.4). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- AGLC: "Retail liquor stores in Alberta are permitted to provide delivery. Contact your local liquor store to see if they can arrange convenient delivery" (search snippet). — [AGLC, Liquor products in Alberta](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-products-alberta)
- CLS: "LTO products are ordered by our customers on a first come, first served basis"; "Unless products are specifically allocated by the Supplier or Agency, all products are sold on a first come, first served basis." — [Connect Logistics, Retailer FAQ](https://connect-logistics.com/frequently-asked-questions-Licensees)

### Inferences
- Use "AGLC product code" (optionally "AGLC product code / CSPC") in copy; "CSPC" alone is legacy wording that AGLC says is "no longer available" for new products.
- Accurate trade vocabulary for Enotri copy: "registered liquor agency", "AGLC", "Connect Logistics Services (CLS)", "Liquor Connect", "AGLC product code", "full cases", "wholesale (licensee) price", "Class A licensees" (restaurants, bars, hotels), "Class D retailers" (liquor stores), "allocation".
- A restaurant does not have to buy from CLS: it can buy Enotri wine from a liquor store that carries it. This matters for small venues that can't reach the 25-case CLS minimum; copy can say "order through Connect Logistics, or through your usual liquor store".
- "Standard Alberta terms" is not a defined term in any source; the real terms are: AGLC wholesale price (same for every licensee), full cases, CLS order minimum and delivery charges, payment to AGLC.
- Enotri does not "handle the paperwork" of the licensee's order: the licensee orders and pays AGLC/CLS directly. Enotri's actual role is registration, keeping stock in the CLS warehouse, pricing, allocations, and following up on availability/out-of-stocks.

- The CLS 2026 Agent & Licensee Rates PDF (order and delivery charges) was linked but not read; exact licensee order/delivery fees and current order-day rules are unconfirmed.
- The CLS Retailer FAQ is undated; the 25-case minimum is long-standing but should be **CONFIRMED** with CLS (1-800-265-6784) and whether it applies identically to Class A licensees and Class D stores.
- Could not open AGLC's consumer page in full (only a search snippet) or the Retail Liquor Store Handbook's special-order section.
- No source checked on whether AGLC/CLS offer licensee credit terms beyond the bank-guarantee arrangement.

## 2. Verdicts on Enotri's current site claims, with corrected wording

### Takeaway
None of the six claims is unlawful as written, but four overstate or blur how the system works. The main fixes: (a) the licensee, not Enotri, places and pays for the order with CLS/AGLC, by the full case; (b) "no minimum" is true for Enotri but CLS applies its own per-delivery case minimum, and venues can also buy from a liquor store; (c) "allocation" is an AGLC trade term and private clients can only buy through a liquor store; (d) sampling and in-person tastings are for licensees, within AGLC per-wine limits.

## 3. What registration an agency needs, and whether to disclose it

### Takeaway
In Alberta the requirement is AGLC **liquor agency registration** (two-year term, applied for through AGLC's Online Licensing Portal), with each supplier appointing the agency by letter and each product registered through the Liquor Agency Portal. Agency staff who pour at tastings or provide samples need **ProServe** certification. I found no separate liquor "representative licence" for Alberta and no rule requiring the registration to be shown on a website.

### Cited Findings
- "If you would like to import and market a manufacturer's or supplier's liquor products you'll need to become a registered liquor agency." Registration is through AGLC's Online Licensing Portal. After registration: Liquor Agency Portal access "so you can register new products and manage your own pricing"; "Product may be purchased at wholesale prices… Product may only be used to provide samples to licensees"; agencies must provide quarterly inbound/outbound forecasts when AGLC asks. — [AGLC, To become a liquor agency](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-agencies-and-suppliers/become-liquor-agency)
- "Agencies must register with AGLC (see AGLC's Fee Schedule at aglc.ca/fee-schedule)" (LA 2.1.1). "A supplier must either appoint a registered agency or register its own company as a liquor agency" (2.1.3). Sub-agents allowed (2.1.4). "An agency registration has a two year term. The registration may be terminated if the agency has not shipped a liquor product to the Alberta marketplace for six months." (2.1.5). "A registration is not transferable." (2.4.1). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- Supplier "Letter of Authorization" and agency "Letter of Understanding" for each supplier (LA 3.1.2–3.1.3); one agency per product at a time (3.1.4). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- ProServe is mandatory for "persons registered with AGLC as a liquor agency and their employees, or a third-party agent acting on their behalf, whose duties include liquor tastings and sampling of liquor products" (LA 2.3.2 d, amended June 2026); proof must be shown to an AGLC inspector on request (2.3.6). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- Independence: "The relationship between a supplier or agency and a licensee must be competitive in nature" (LA 2.5.1); licensees and their employees cannot own, manage, advise or finance an agency (2.5.2–2.5.4). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- The handbook defines "staff" as anyone "employed by an agency/supplier or contracted by an agency, supplier or third party" (LA 1.2.1 o); "agency" in the advertising and promotion sections "includes all associated shareholders, directors, management, agents and employees" (LA 4.1.1 b, 5.1.1 a). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- For comparison, AGLC's cannabis rules do use a "cannabis representative" category (2025 rule changes). — [StratCann, AGLC announces changes to cannabis promotion rules](https://stratcann.com/news/aglc-announces-changes-to-cannabis-promotion-rules/)
- Ontario practice (not an Alberta rule): Lifford's FAQ "fine print" discloses its role: "Lifford is not permitted to sell beverage alcohol but may arrange for purchases from the LCBO on the customer's behalf." — [Lifford FAQ](https://lifford.com/pages/faq)

### Inferences
- Copy should say "registered liquor agency" (AGLC's own term), not "licensed importer" or "liquor representative". AGLC, not Enotri, is the importer of record, so "imports wines from South Africa and Europe" is fine as marketing shorthand but "importer of record" claims should be avoided.
- A one-line role disclosure in the footer or FAQ is good practice and pre-empts "can I buy from you" questions: e.g. "Enotri Wine Imports is an AGLC-registered liquor agency. We don't sell wine directly; all sales in Alberta are made through AGLC and licensed retailers."
- Because "agency" includes employees and contractors in the advertising/promotion rules, any contractor running Enotri's social media or tastings is bound by the same rules.

## 4. Rules on advertising, promotion, sampling and inducements that affect the website

### Takeaway
AGLC's Liquor Agency Handbook explicitly treats websites, social posts and email as "advertising". Website copy must be accurate and verifiable, must not appeal to minors (under 18), encourage non-drinkers, imply health benefits or show more than one drink per person, and responsible-drinking messaging is "highly recommended". Sampling and tastings are tightly limited, free liquor and cash-like benefits to licensees are banned, and sampling events for the trade cannot be publicly advertised. No age gate is required by AGLC, and the CRTC broadcast code does not formally govern an Alberta website, though its principles mirror AGLC's.

### Cited Findings

**Websites count as advertising**
- "advertising"… includes "new media, including but not limited to digital and social media advertising, organic social media posts on newsfeeds and/or profiles, email, search results, blogs, websites or short message service (SMS)" (LA 4.1.1 a). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)
- An agency "may advertise in any medium not specifically prohibited, as long as the advertising complies with these policies, the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC) and any other regulator with jurisdiction" (LA 4.1.2). — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Content rules (LA 4.1–4.2)**
- "All advertising must be: a) accurate and verifiable; and b) within the limits of good taste and propriety" (4.1.3).
- Advertising must not "encourage non-drinkers to consume liquor"; "promote irresponsible liquor consumption or service"; "show heavy or prolonged liquor consumption"; "give the impression liquor benefits a person's health"; or "disparage (put down) another company, business or product" (4.1.4).
- Minors: "Advertising must not appeal to minors or be placed in any medium targeted specifically at minors"; no minor "or anyone who may reasonably be mistaken for a minor may appear"; no children's fairy tales, nursery rhymes or characters (4.1.5).
- Endorsements by a well-known personality "should not imply that drinking liquor contributed to their success" (4.1.6).
- An agency "is not allowed to pay any advertising costs for a licensee, either directly or indirectly" (4.1.9); licensees need the agency's permission to use its brand logos and may not receive any benefit for advertising its brands (4.1.10).
- "Advertising which promotes the responsible consumption of liquor (i.e., legal, moderate and safe) is highly recommended and supported by AGLC." (4.1.11)
- Drinking scenes: "The quantity of a liquor product shown in a social setting must not exceed one drink per person"; no liquor before or while driving or doing anything dangerous (4.2.1). A bottle may be shown where drinking isn't normally allowed only as "a beauty shot" with no people (4.2.2 a); "The presence of food is encouraged" (4.2.2 c).
- Contests sponsored by a supplier "must be directed only towards persons of legal drinking age" (4.3.12 b).
- Product registration: AGLC may refuse products "likely to appeal to minors" (3.1.7, amended Sep 2026); organic products need "organic" in the registered name (3.1.9 c).
- All of the above — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Inducements to licensees (LA 5.2–5.3)**
- "An agency is prohibited from directing any promotional activity or items to a licensee that could directly benefit the licensee or their staff" (5.2.1); no participation in licensee loyalty programs (5.2.3).
- An agency may not offer licensees "cash, rebates, coupons or credits of any monetary value", account deposits, or "free liquor products, other than for sampling purposes as specified in Section 5.6", nor pay for décor, furniture, equipment (5.2.6).
- Allowed education expenses: travel to a winery the agency represents, conference/seminar fees, and "dinners/events designed to increase awareness of the agency's products" (5.2.4); not "activities that are recreational or leisurely in nature" (5.2.5).
- Allowed non-essential items include glasses, corkscrews, menu printing, branded signs without the licensee's name (5.2.13); tickets with conditions (5.2.8).
- "Cash, cheques, gift cards or prepaid credit cards are prohibited as promotional items" (5.3.3); promotional agreements "cannot result in excluding or prohibiting any competitor's product(s)" (5.3.4).
- Private labels cannot be used "as an inducement… whereby they receive a premium product at a discounted price" (3.11.10 c).
- All — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Tastings and sampling (LA 5.5–5.7, 3.16–3.17)**
- Licensee sampling: per brand, wine max "4 X 750 ml bottles, or equivalent"; "for consumption by the licensee and cannot be sold or provided to patrons"; bought from AGLC or a Class D store at licensee cost (not from the same licensee being sampled); records for two years; "A licensee may be provided with samples of a specific product only twice per calendar year" (5.6.1).
- No-sale sampling functions: "Attendance must be by invitation only"; "No public advertising is allowed"; unlicensed location needs a Private Non-Sale Special Event licence; agency office needs a hospitality licence (5.6.2). Hospitality-room licensees "must not advertise hosting activities or the providing of samples" (4.6.8).
- Consumer tastings on licensed premises: max wine pour "56 ml (2 oz)"; no minors; no intoxication; ProServe server (5.5.1–5.5.2); "Agencies are not licensed to sell liquor and as a result may not charge a fee to customers for liquor tastings" (5.5.3); agency must be present and serve, product bought from the licensee (5.5.4); not outside the licensed premises (5.5.5); Class D stores can pour on the agency's behalf under a written Promotional Agreement, staffing costs capped at $25/hour (5.5.6).
- Public samples: one per adult per day, wine max "One - 200 ml bottle", handed over in person or via a Class D delivery licensee, "Samples cannot be mailed", marked "not for resale" and "for off-site consumption only", bought through AGLC at full price (5.7.1).
- Vouchers: an agency may give the public vouchers redeemable at liquor stores, wine max "one x 750 ml bottle"; receipt must not be conditional on purchase (5.8.3).
- Charity: an agency may donate liquor or run free tastings for a bona fide charitable fundraiser on licensed premises (5.4.2).
- Samples from the producer (for unregistered/new products): up to "eight - 750 ml or six - 1,000 ml bottles of wine" free of AGLC markup, shipped DAP to AGLC's sample room or c/o AGLC, with notice to agency.samples@aglc.ca (3.16.1–3.16.9). Wine bought at wholesale by an agency may only be for "the personal use of licensees or their staff" or approved charity events, never sold, never shipped out of Alberta (3.17.1).
- All — [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Drinking age**
- Alberta: "'Minor' means a person under the age of 18 years" (LH 1.2.1 m); staff must be at least 18 to serve; staff must ask for ID from anyone who appears under 25 (LA 2.3.7 c references "persons who appear to be under 25 years of age"). — [AGLC Liquor Licensee Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf); [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)

**Ad Standards / CRTC Code**
- Ad Standards' Aug 2025 guide: "The CRTC Code applies to all broadcast advertising of alcoholic beverages in Canada." For print, out-of-home, streaming, banners, "Social Media Posts, Websites, etc.", its media-market table lists only Ontario (AGCO Guidelines) and British Columbia ("In British Columbia, the CRTC Code applies to all advertising of alcoholic beverages (e.g. broadcast, print and digital advertising)"); Alberta is not listed. Quebec requires preclearance by its liquor board. — [Ad Standards, Alcoholic Beverage Advertising Clearance Guide (Aug 2025)](https://adstandards.ca/wp-content/uploads/AlcoholicBeverageAdClearanceGuideEN-2025-Aug.pdf)
- CRTC Code objective as quoted by Ad Standards: advertising "does not contribute to the negative health and societal effects relating to excessive or inappropriate alcohol consumption" and "must not be designed to promote the general consumption of alcoholic beverages"; messages "must not… be directed to persons under the legal drinking age"; "It is recommended that all actors be, and appear to be, over the age of 25." — [Ad Standards guide](https://adstandards.ca/wp-content/uploads/AlcoholicBeverageAdClearanceGuideEN-2025-Aug.pdf)
- Full code text: [CRTC, Code for Broadcast Advertising of Alcoholic Beverages](https://crtc.gc.ca/Eng/Television/Publicit/Codesalco.Htm) (not opened; referenced from search).

### Inferences
- Website implications for Enotri:
  - Every factual claim (ratings, "organic", "sustainable", awards, "only importer of X", price) must be "accurate and verifiable"; organic claims should match how the product is registered with AGLC.
  - Imagery: one glass per person, food on the table, adults who look 25+, no driving/boating/skiing with wine, no youth cues.
  - Do not publicise trade tasting events or "free samples" on the public site; describe tastings as a service for licensed buyers ("by appointment") rather than as events or giveaways. Consumer-facing events should be framed as being at a licensed venue or store, with no fee charged by Enotri for the wine.
  - Do not offer licensees anything of monetary value on the site (e.g. "first order discount", "free case with your first order", rebates). Since price must be the same for all licensees regardless of quantity (LA 3.8.1), avoid "volume pricing" or "trade discounts" language.
  - Listing venues that pour Enotri wines is not prohibited, but Enotri may not pay for a licensee's advertising (LA 4.1.9). **CONFIRM** with AGLC if Enotri plans a "where to drink us" map that promotes specific licensees.
- Age gate: not required by any AGLC source found. Many Canadian agencies use one anyway (see section 6). A light age statement in the footer ("For adults of legal drinking age, 18+ in Alberta") satisfies the spirit of LA 4.1.5 without a pop-up; an age gate is optional.
- Prices to consumers: no AGLC prohibition found on an agency publishing wholesale prices, but retail prices are set by stores (CLS FAQ), so any consumer-facing price should be labelled as approximate/suggested or omitted. **CONFIRM**.
- CRTC Code: formally broadcast-only in Alberta, but AGLC LA 4.1.2 says advertising must comply with the CRTC "and any other regulator with jurisdiction", so following the Code's principles on the website is the safe default.

### Gaps
- Did not review the Competition Act / Competition Bureau guidance on misleading representations or environmental claims (likely covered by the sustainability researcher).
- Did not find AGLC guidance specific to e-commerce-style price displays by agencies.
- The Retail Liquor Store Handbook's advertising section (relevant if Enotri co-promotes with a store) not read.

## 5. Privacy (PIPA/PIPEDA) and anti-spam (CASL) for the contact form and any newsletter

### Takeaway
Enotri's contact form is governed by Alberta's **PIPA** (not PIPEDA) for collection inside Alberta; it needs a short notice at the point of collection stating the purpose and a named contact. A newsletter needs **CASL** express opt-in consent (no pre-ticked boxes), sender identification and a working unsubscribe in every email; replying to an enquiry is fine, and the enquiry itself gives six months of implied consent.

### Cited Findings

**Which privacy law**
- Alberta's PIPA, BC's PIPA and Quebec's private-sector act are "substantially similar" to PIPEDA; organizations subject to them are generally exempt from PIPEDA for collection, use or disclosure within that province, but PIPEDA still applies to personal information that crosses provincial or national borders. — [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, Provincial laws that may apply instead of PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/legislation-related-to-pipeda/provincial-legislation-deemed-substantially-similar-to-pipeda/) (seen via search summary)

**PIPA notice and consent (Government of Alberta guidance)**
- "Before or while collecting the information, the organization must: give notice that it intends to collect personal information and provide the purposes for the collection; give the name, position or title of the person who is able to answer the individual's questions." — [Alberta.ca, Collecting personal information](https://www.alberta.ca/collecting-personal-information)
- Someone is "deemed to consent" if they "voluntarily provide the information to the organization and it is reasonable for that purpose"; organizations can only use it "for the purposes provided in the notice and to the extent reasonable"; "An organization may not provide false or misleading information in its notice." — [Alberta.ca, Collecting personal information](https://www.alberta.ca/collecting-personal-information)
- If a service provider outside Canada is used to collect information, the organization must say how to access its policies about that provider and who can answer questions. — [Alberta.ca, Collecting personal information](https://www.alberta.ca/collecting-personal-information)
- "Business contact information" is listed among publicly available information that may be collected without consent in some cases. — [Alberta.ca, Collecting personal information](https://www.alberta.ca/collecting-personal-information)

**CASL (CRTC FAQ, modified 26 Feb 2026)**
- Three requirements for a commercial electronic message (CEM): "(1) obtain consent, (2) provide identification information, and (3) provide an unsubscribe mechanism." — [CRTC, CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)
- A message is a CEM if one purpose is "to encourage the recipient to participate in a commercial activity"; a logo or contact details in a signature alone "does not necessarily make an email a CEM", but a promotional tagline would. — [CRTC, CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)
- "Express consent does not expire"; the sender must prove consent. "You cannot use a pre-checked box to request consent… The end user must take a positive action to indicate their consent." — [CRTC, CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)
- An inquiry triggers a "six-month period during which consent to receive commercial electronic messages (CEMs) is implied" (CASL para. 10(10)(e)); a purchase triggers two years (10(10)(a)). CRTC advice: "It is best not to assume implied consent: if you are unsure, ask for express consent." — [CRTC, CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)
- Business-to-business exemption: CASL does not apply to CEMs between employees/representatives of organizations that "have a relationship" where "the message concerns the activities of the organization to which the message is sent". Conspicuously published business addresses can imply consent only for messages "relevant to the person's business, role, functions or duties". — [CRTC, CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)
- Unsubscribe requests must be honoured without delay and no later than 10 business days (secondary). — [Email Sender & Provider Coalition, CASL overview](https://www.espcoalition.org/sites/default/files/resources/Overview_CASL_Innovation_ESPC_4-25-11%281%29.pdf)
- Government of Canada consent guidance. — [fightspam.gc.ca, Getting consent to send email](https://www.fightspam.gc.ca/eic/site/030.nsf/eng/00008.html) (seen via search summary)
- Example newsletter consent line used by an Ontario agency: "Yes, I would like to receive emails from Lifford Cellars. I understand that I can unsubscribe at any time." — [Lifford FAQ](https://lifford.com/pages/faq)

### Inferences
- The current mailto: form still collects personal information (name, venue, buyer type, message) in Enotri's inbox, so PIPA applies. Minimal notice under the form:
  > "We use your details only to reply to your enquiry and arrange tastings or samples. We don't sell or share them. Questions about your information: Rawlings Munemo, rallings@enotri.ca."
- If the mailbox or any form/newsletter tool stores data outside Canada (e.g. Google Workspace, Mailchimp, Formspree), add: "Our email and newsletter providers may store information outside Canada." and say how to ask about it (PIPA service-provider rule). **CONFIRM** with a privacy adviser.
- Minimal privacy page sections: who we are and contact; what we collect (enquiry details, newsletter email); why; how long kept; providers and where data is stored; how to access/correct or withdraw consent; date updated.
- Newsletter: separate, unticked checkbox ("Send me Enotri's occasional trade news and new arrivals. You can unsubscribe at any time."), record the date/time/source of each sign-up, and every email must carry Enotri's name, a mailing address plus email/phone/web, and a one-click unsubscribe.
- Replying to a trade enquiry is not spam. Follow-up marketing to that person is OK for six months on implied consent; after that, ask for express consent. B2B emails to venues Enotri already works with likely fall under the B2B exemption, but keeping express consent is simpler.
- Because the audience includes private clients, the newsletter sign-up should include an age statement ("I am 18 or older").
