# Enotri: The Ground Before the Label

## Creative authority

Interviewed brief based on Rawlings Munemo's answers.

## 1. Vibe and references

**Vibe:** Sun-warmed, grounded, unhurried, earned.

**References:** Kermit Lynch Wine Merchant, *A Good Year* (Ridley Scott), and Cereal Magazine.

## 2. Scroll journey

1. **Land:** A quiet title page gives way to vineyard rows and the soil before the label.
2. **Growers:** The visitor meets the people and hands behind the fruit.
3. **Fruit and process:** Ripeness, harvest, and cellar work show how the raw material becomes wine.
4. **Estate:** Stone, history, and light form the immersive peak, where Enotri's founder story is revealed.
5. **Invitation:** The page settles into a warm invitation to view the portfolio, request a price list, or plan a wine event.

## 3. Energy curve

Calm title page and land opening. The hero builds gently through growers and process. The Estate chapter is the only fully cinematic peak. Portfolio and contact settle back into documentary quiet.

## 4. Feeling curve

- **Land:** Stillness and quiet awe, caused by an unhurried vineyard photograph and sparse caption.
- **Growers:** Respect and quiet trust, caused by close human-work imagery and factual labels.
- **Fruit / process:** Gentle anticipation, caused by harvest and cellar details moving into sequence.
- **Estate:** Arrival and earned access, caused by the architectural reveal and the founder story.
- **Invitation:** Warm release, caused by a calm table scene and an open-handed contact line.

## 5. Peak

“The whole site felt like walking through someone's family history before it told you that the founder is the first Black-owned wine agency doing this.”

The peak lives in the Estate chapter.

## 6. Aesthetic range

Editorial-leaning premium-minimal, with occasional retro warmth. Never brutalist, maximalist, or dense.

## 7. Structure

Distinct chapters with deliberate hard cuts. The page should not feel like one continuous world. Each chapter has its own ground, image, and museum-label copy.

## 8. Existing assets

The project already contains vineyard, château, grower, harvest, cellar, producer, bottle, and Enotri logo imagery. More images may be added later. AI-generated visuals are allowed when an existing image does not cover a needed scene.

## Journey and score

| Beat | Device | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Title page | `flow` + `in` | A printed title page establishes the editorial contract without an over-produced hero. |
| Land | `reveal` | The first photograph arrives as a chapter boundary and leaves the title page behind. |
| Growers | `parallax` | Separate caption and image movement gives the human work a physical presence. |
| Fruit / process | `flow` + `in` | A quiet sequence of factual details reads like a magazine spread. |
| Estate | `reveal` + bespoke depth | The hard cut and layered image create the single emotional peak. |
| Invitation | `flow` + `in` | The close resolves as a colophon and running invitation rather than a button island. |

## Signature move

The **terroir trace** is a fixed, narrow contour line at the bottom of the viewport. As the visitor passes each chapter, the line draws another section and stamps the region name into the ledger. It is both a memory of the journey and a quiet index of the growers' places, not a generic progress bar.

## Tell-someone sentence

“It's the site where you walk from the soil to the estate, then discover the person who brought those growers to Alberta.”

## Authored silence

The land and invitation chapters intentionally contain pauses with very little copy. These are authored moments, not dead scroll.

## Homepage rebuild direction

The homepage was reskinned around the supplied Fora reference while keeping Enotri-specific content throughout:

- Dark charcoal foundation with terracotta, burnt umber, muted gold, and dusty rose atmosphere.
- Warm serif display type paired with a neutral sans-serif body face.
- Navigation, hero, portfolio browser, intro, four-tab core features, three alternating feature blocks, three-step workflow, FAQ, journal, grower spotlight, and footer.
- Interactive portfolio tabs and native FAQ accordions.
- Rolling vineyard silhouettes replace the reference site's mountain silhouettes.
