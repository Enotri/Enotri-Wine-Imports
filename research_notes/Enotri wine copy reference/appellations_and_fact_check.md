# Appellation Terminology and Producer Fact-Check for Enotri Wine Imports (current to 15 October 2026)

Method note: pages were read with Firecrawl (server-side) and web search. Direct fetches from this environment were blocked, and Firecrawl had a shared rate limit, so a few findings rest on search excerpts or importer pages rather than the producer's own site. These are marked "(secondary)". Quotes are kept to 25 words or fewer and are attributed. Verdict scale for Part B: **Confirmed / Partly correct / Incorrect / Could not verify**.

## B1. Pasetti (Abruzzo): generations, altitude, Pecorino, wines, organic

### Takeaway
The family is in its fifth generation and the vineyards are high, but the move to altitude dates from about 2000. The estate is not certified organic, and Madonnella is not a Riserva.

### Cited Findings
- **Five generations: Partly correct.** Decanter describes it as "now run by the fifth generation of the family"; Callmewine says the business "passed down for 4 generations" (which would make the current one the fifth). — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/?p=473996); [Callmewine](https://us.callmewine.com/winery/pasetti-B1172.htm)
- **"Farmed at altitude for five generations": Incorrect.** "More than two decades ago, the Pasetti family made the decision to sell their vineyards near the sea in Francavilla and buy inland vineyard sites at high altitudes." The family's origin is coastal: "Silvestro Pasetti bought land in Francavilla… and planted vineyards there." — [Do Bianchi (J. Parzen), 2024](https://dobianchi.com/2024/09/05/the-wonderful-high-altitude-wines-of-pasetti-in-abruzzo-are-driven-by-the-life-force-of-family/); [Callmewine](https://us.callmewine.com/winery/pasetti-B1172.htm)
- **450–600 m on the slopes of the Gran Sasso: Partly correct.** Capestrano is at 450 m and Pescosansonesco at 550 m (33 ha). — [Crushed Grape Chronicles](https://www.crushedgrapechronicles.com/pasetti-in-the-gran-sasso-laga-mountains-national-park-of-abruzzo/). The producer's own page says Collecivetta "grows in Pescosansonesco at 550 metres… at the foot of Gran Sasso." — [Pasetti](https://www.pasettivini.it/en/products/white-wines/colle-civetta/). Decanter reports Pecorino plantings "up to a remarkable 1,000m" near Pescosansonesco. — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/wine/abruzzo-for-wine-lovers-525615/). About 70 ha "between Gran Sasso and the Monti della Laga, in the heart of the National Park". — [Callmewine](https://us.callmewine.com/winery/pasetti-B1172.htm)
- **"Helped revive Pecorino": Could not verify.** Decanter credits the mid-1990s Pecorino revival chiefly to Cataldi Madonna (first bottled in 1996). — [Decanter, via search](https://www.decanter.com/sponsored/wines-of-abruzzo-rediscovering-pecorino-473996)
- **Collecivetta (Pecorino): Confirmed.** The producer page is titled "Collecivetta" (URL slug "colle-civetta"). Decanter reviewed "Colle Civetta Pecorino Superiore 2020". — [Pasetti](https://www.pasettivini.it/en/products/white-wines/colle-civetta/); [Decanter, via search](https://www.decanter.com/?p=473996)
- **Testarossa as Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Riserva: Confirmed.** Listed as "Montepulciano d'Abruzzo 'Testarossa' Riserva Pasetti 2021". The Testarossa line "was created to celebrate the beauty of the red-haired women of the Pasetti family" and also includes a Rosato and a Trebbiano d'Abruzzo. — [Callmewine](https://us.callmewine.com/winery/pasetti-B1172.htm); [Crushed Grape Chronicles](https://www.crushedgrapechronicles.com/pasetti-in-the-gran-sasso-laga-mountains-national-park-of-abruzzo/)
- **Madonnella as a Riserva: Incorrect.** It is "their classic Montepulciano d'Abruzzo", aged in concrete and large oak, and Vinmonopolet lists it as Montepulciano d'Abruzzo with no Riserva. — [Crushed Grape Chronicles](https://www.crushedgrapechronicles.com/pasetti-in-the-gran-sasso-laga-mountains-national-park-of-abruzzo/); [Vinmonopolet](https://www.vinmonopolet.no/p/19155101)
- **"Organic": Incorrect.** The producer's FAQ: "I nostri vini sono certificati SQNPI – Sistema di Qualità Nazionale di Produzione Integrata… Siamo inoltre in conversione al biologico." — [Pasetti store FAQ](https://www.pasettistore.com/)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Pasetti, a fifth-generation Abruzzo family, moved its vineyards inland more than two decades ago, to 450–550 m and above at Pescosansonesco and Capestrano, inside the Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park. Certified integrated production (SQNPI); in conversion to organic."
- "Rocco Pasetti / Contesa" (Collecorvino) is a separate estate. Do not merge the two in copy.

### Gaps
- No primary source for an official Pasetti generation count or a "Pecorino pioneer" role.

## B2. Cantina Zaccagnini: founding and place

### Takeaway
Confirmed. Since 2023, though, the winery has been majority-owned by Argea.

### Cited Findings
- **Founded 1978 in Bolognano by Marcello Zaccagnini: Confirmed.** The press kit says "Since its foundation by Marcello Zaccagnini in 1978". It describes the area as the "Pescara valley", "the wild beauty of the Maiella and Monte Morrone" and a "wide coastline overlooking the Adriatic Sea". The press kit gives 300 ha. — [Zaccagnini press kit 2025 (PDF)](https://www.cantinazaccagnini.it/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/04/Zaccagnini-PK_2025_ENG_LR.pdf); [Zaccagnini site](https://www.cantinazaccagnini.it/en/)
- **Ownership:** "OWNERSHIP: Argea 80% - famiglia Zaccagnini 20%"; acquired in 2023. — [Zaccagnini press kit](https://www.cantinazaccagnini.it/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/04/Zaccagnini-PK_2025_ENG_LR.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Between the Majella and the Adriatic" is a fair summary. The producer spells it "Maiella"; "Majella" is also standard (it is the National Park's own spelling).
- Do not describe the winery as "family-owned".

### Gaps
- None material.

## B4. Corte Moschina (Roncà, Soave and Colli Berici)

### Takeaway
Family, place, soils and wine names are confirmed. "Organic" is not supported by the producer's own wording.

### Cited Findings
- **Family estate in Roncà: Confirmed.** "In 1998… the Danese-Niero family took the property in hand"; the estate is run by "Silvano and Patrizia, Alessandro and Giacomo". It grew from five to 35 ha "distributed in four different areas between Verona and Vicenza". — [Wine Paris exhibitor profile](https://wineparis.com/newfront/exhibitor/corte-moschina)
- **Basalt volcanic soils at the foot of the Monti Lessini: Confirmed.** The producer cites "the Calvarina Hill's basaltic soil" and the "Lessini Hills". — [Corte Moschina](https://www.cortemoschina.it/en). The Soave consortium lists a "Roncà – Monte Calvarina" UGA. — [Consorzio Soave UGA map](https://www.ilsoave.com/en/the-uga-map/)
- **Colli Berici limestone: Partly correct (wording).** The producer cites "Alonte's iron and calcareous stones" and the "Berici Hills". — [Corte Moschina (search excerpt and scrape)](https://www.cortemoschina.it/en)
- **Organic: Could not verify, and probably incorrect.** The producer "follows the principles of integrated family management". One Quebec agent's page flags Roncathe 2024 as organic. — [Wine Paris profile](https://wineparis.com/newfront/exhibitor/corte-moschina); contradicted by [Trinque (Quebec agent)](https://trinque.kork.ca/qc/en/our-wineries/corte-moschina/roncathe-soave/2024/14954155)
- **Wines: Confirmed.** "Roncathe Soave DOC di Roncà"; "Granetto Pinot Grigio DOC Delle Venezie"; "Calcàreo", Carménère, Colli Berici. — [Corte Moschina](https://www.cortemoschina.it/en); [Calcàreo page](https://www.cortemoschina.it/en/calcareo)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "A family estate in Roncà, restored by the Danese-Niero family from 1998, on the basalt of Monte Calvarina at the eastern edge of the Lessini hills, with further vineyards on the iron-rich limestone of Alonte in the Colli Berici." Leave out "organic" unless a certificate is supplied.
- The Colli Berici consortium spells the grape "Carmenère"; the producer writes "Carménère". Pick one spelling and use it everywhere.

### Gaps
- No certification record found.

## B5. Stellenrust (Stellenbosch)

### Takeaway
The old-vine Chenin on decomposed granite in Bottelary is confirmed for one block. "Fourth generation" and WIETA/IPW could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Fourth generation: Could not verify.** The producer says only "carrying through generations of winemaking skills" and "one of the largest family owned wine estates in South Africa". — [Stellenrust history](https://www.stellenrust.co.za/ourhistory). The fourth-generation Bottelary estate in trade coverage is Kaapzicht. — [Wine Anorak / search](https://wineanorak.com/2022/11/01/on-the-chenin-blanc-trail-in-stellenbosch-visiting-four-old-vineyards-and-tasting-the-wines/)
- **Bottelary Hills: Partly correct.** The estate farms "nearly 400ha", "From the decomposed granite of the Helderberg's Golden Triangle to the sandy clay soils of the maritime Bottelary Hills". — [Stellenrust history](https://www.stellenrust.co.za/ourhistory)
- **Old bush-vine Chenin on decomposed granite: Confirmed for the specific block.** The producer: "43-year-old bush vines from Bottelary property planted to decomposed granite–dryland vineyards"; "Block 28 is planted on North Eastern slopes of decomposed granite." — [Stellenrust Old Bush Vine Chenin](https://www.stellenrust.co.za/curatorslimitededition/stellenrust-old-bush-vine-chenin-blanc)
- **Planted in the 1960s: Partly correct.** A 2022 visit found a Stellenrust block that was "58 years old, which would make it 1964 plantings". — [Wine Anorak (secondary)](https://wineanorak.com/2022/11/01/on-the-chenin-blanc-trail-in-stellenbosch-visiting-four-old-vineyards-and-tasting-the-wines/). The producer's undated page says "43-year-old"; a retailer lists another Bottelary Chenin as planted in 1983. — [Systembolaget](https://www.systembolaget.se/produkt/vin/stellenrust-secrets-lies-chenin-blanc-2006801/)
- **WIETA & IPW: Could not verify.** The producer cites Fairtrade: "This project enabled the farms to become Fairtrade certified" (a 72-worker empowerment project). — [Stellenrust history](https://www.stellenrust.co.za/ourhistory)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "One of South Africa's largest family-owned estates, Stellenrust farms close to 400 ha across the Helderberg and the Bottelary Hills. Its Old Bush Vine Chenin comes from dryland bush vines on decomposed granite at Bottelary. Fairtrade certified." Quote the planting year from the label or SAWIS record (the Certified Heritage Vineyards seal shows it).
- The official Wine of Origin ward is "Bottelary". "Bottelary Hills" is the estate's and the wine route's usage.

### Gaps
- Generation count; WIETA and IPW status (check the WIETA and SWSA/IPW member lists).

## B6. Bosman Family Vineyards (Wellington)

### Takeaway
Generations, worker equity and Fairtrade are confirmed. "Largest vine nursery" should be replaced by the producer's own market-share figure.

### Cited Findings
- **Eight generations: Confirmed.** "The Bosman Family have been vine growers in Wellington for 8 generations." The producer gives 1810 for the settlement at Lelienfontein; Wikipedia says 1798. — [Bosman Our Story](https://bosmanwines.com/our-story/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosman_Family_Vineyards)
- **"South Africa's largest vine nursery": Partly correct.** The producer: "The Bosman Adama vine nursery produces more than 30% of the grafted cuttings for the South African wine industry"; also "more than one third of the country's grafted vine cuttings". — [Bosman clonal selection page](https://bosmanwines.com/environmental-sustainability/clonal-selections-and-study/); [Adama Red tech sheet (PDF)](https://bosmanwines.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Adama-Red-2023-Publisher-Document.pdf). Wikipedia: "one of the most influential vine nurseries in Africa". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosman_Family_Vineyards)
- **Worker equity scheme: Confirmed.** In 2008 a joint venture with the Adama Workers Trust gave eligible workers "co-ownership of 430ha"; workers "together own 26% of the business". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosman_Family_Vineyards); [Bosman Adama](https://bosmanadama.co.za/our-story/)
- **Fairtrade: Confirmed.** Certified in 2009. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosman_Family_Vineyards); [Bosman awards page](https://bosmanwines.com/awards/bosman-family-vineyards-recognised-for-excellence/)
- **Context:** WOSA says Wellington "supplies over 85% of the South African wine industry with cuttings". — [WOSA](https://www.wosa.co.za/The-Industry/Winegrowing-Areas/Winelands-of-South-Africa/)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Eight generations of vine growers in Wellington; their Bosman Adama nursery supplies more than a third of South Africa's grafted vine cuttings. Co-owned with its workers since 2008; Fairtrade certified since 2009."

### Gaps
- The 1798 vs 1810 settlement date conflict.

## B8. Domaine des Ardoisières (Savoie)

### Takeaway
The terraces are at Cévins in the Tarentaise valley, not above Fréterive; Fréterive is the cellar address. The wines are IGP Vin des Allobroges, not Savoie AOP.

### Cited Findings
- **"Reclaimed abandoned schist terraces above Fréterive": Incorrect location.** "Founded in 1998 by Michel Grisard as the replanting of the Cevins hillside". — [Millesima](https://www.millesima-usa.com/producer-domaine-des-ardoisieres.html). The project began in 1997 with a Cévins municipal initiative. — [Millesima / iDealwine (secondary)](https://www.idealwine.net/domaine-des-ardoisieres-le-nouvel-elan-de-la-savoie). The domaine's address is "Le Villard, 72, impasse de la Pierre Marquée, 73250 Fréterive". — [Parc des Bauges listing (PDF)](https://geotrek.parcdesbauges.com/api/fr/touristiccontents/321/domaine-des-ardoisieres.pdf). It also farms Saint-Pierre-de-Soucy, on "schistous marl from the Jurassic period". — [iDealwine (secondary)](https://www.idealwine.net/domaine-des-ardoisieres-le-nouvel-elan-de-la-savoie)
- **Brice Omont: Confirmed.** He runs the domaine (sources date his takeover between 2005 and 2010). — [Search summary of importer pages](https://www.idealwine.info/?p=6536)
- **Jacquère, Mondeuse, Persan: Partly correct.** Améthyste is Persan and Mondeuse Noire; Argile Rouge is Gamay and Persan; Quartz is Altesse. Jacquère was not found in the sources read. — [Retailer/importer listings (secondary)](https://closcachet.com.au/products/domaine-des-ardoisieres-amethyste)
- **Organic: Confirmed.** "Certified organic (Ecocert) practising biodynamic"; slopes up to 60%, at 400–500 m. — [Vine Trail (UK importer)](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres/domaine-des-ardoisieres-quartz-2019-vin-des-allobroges-igp)
- **Appellation:** The wines are sold as "IGP Vin des Allobroges". — [Vine Trail](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres/domaine-des-ardoisieres-quartz-2019-vin-des-allobroges-igp); [Sip Wines](https://sipwines.shop/products/argile-rouge-igp-vin-des-allobroges-domaine-des-ardoisieres-2022)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Brice Omont farms schist terraces at Cévins in the Tarentaise valley, replanted from 1997–98 after decades of abandonment, plus slopes at Saint-Pierre-de-Soucy. Persan, Mondeuse, Altesse and Gamay. Certified organic, farmed biodynamically. IGP Vin des Allobroges."
- Cévins is not one of the 16 Vin de Savoie crus (see the Savoie section in Part A).

### Gaps
- Jacquère in the current range; the exact year Omont took over.

- Rootstock status; current hectares (6 vs about 20).

### Takeaway

## A1. Italy: official names, tiers and terroir wording

### Takeaway
Use the consortium's own forms: "Soave DOC / Soave Classico DOC / Soave Superiore DOCG"; "Langhe Nebbiolo DOC"; "Abruzzo (or d'Abruzzo) DOC Pecorino"; "Colline Teramane Montepulciano d'Abruzzo DOCG"; "Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi Classico Superiore DOC" and "Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva DOCG"; and "Fiano di Avellino / Greco di Tufo / Taurasi DOCG". Two rule changes to note: Abruzzo's 2023 Superiore and sub-zone tier, and the Irpinia Riserva categories approved in 2020.

### Cited Findings
**Soave**
- The pyramid is Soave DOC, Soave Classico DOC (the historic hill core), then Soave Superiore DOCG (created 2001, from the 2002 vintage). Recioto di Soave has been DOCG since 1998; Garganega is at least 70%. — [Wine With Seth (secondary)](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/soave-doc/)
- Consortium quote: "The volcanic soils compose a large portion of the hillside production of Soave wines". — [Consorzio Tutela Vini Soave](https://www.ilsoave.com/en/soils/)
- Consortium quote: "The hills with a decidedly basalt substrate are in the eastern area with altitudes that vary from 50 to 500 meters above sea level." — [Consorzio Soave](https://www.ilsoave.com/en/soils/)
- The UGAs (Unità Geografiche Aggiuntive) "cover roughly 38% of the 7.000 hectares of vineyards"; "Roncà – Monte Calvarina" is one of them. — [Consorzio Soave UGA map](https://www.ilsoave.com/en/the-uga-map/)
- FAO named the "Soave Traditional Vineyards" a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System (GIAHS) on 30 November 2018. This is FAO, not UNESCO. — [FAO GIAHS](https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/italy-soave-traditional-system/en); [Harpers](https://www.harpers.co.uk/news/fullstory.php/aid/24469/UN_recognises_vineyards_of_Soave_as_a_Globally_Important_Agricultural_Heritage_System.html)

**Colli Berici DOC**
- Consortium quote (Italian): "Simbolo dell'enologia berica l'autoctono Tai Rosso, i bordolesi Cabernet e Merlot e il Carmenère". On Carmenère: it "è giunto sui Berici nel XIX secolo". — [Consorzio Tutela Vini DOC Colli Berici e Vicenza](https://collibericivicenzadoc.it/vini/)
- Soils are marly limestone, calcareous conglomerates, red clays and some basalt, with karst features. — [The Drinks Business (secondary)](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2023/09/colli-berici-a-hidden-gem-in-the-veneto-region/)

**Langhe DOC / Langhe Nebbiolo**
- Consortium quote: "Il Langhe è una denominazione di tipo territoriale" (a territorial denomination covering "96 comuni" in Langhe and Roero). — [Consorzio Barolo Barbaresco Alba Langhe e Dogliani](https://www.langhevini.it/le-denominazioni-tutelate-dal-consorzio/langhe-doc/)
- Consortium quote on soils: "suoli marnosi, calcarei, sabbiosi ed argillosi, a volte miscelati tra loro in maniera unica". — [langhevini.it](https://www.langhevini.it/le-denominazioni-tutelate-dal-consorzio/langhe-doc/)
- The disciplinare names the type as "Langhe" Nebbiolo. — [DOC Langhe disciplinare (PDF)](https://www.langhevini.it/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/DOC-Langhe.pdf). Minimum 85% Nebbiolo; no minimum ageing. — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/premium/langhe-nebbiolo-20-wines-to-seek-out-529126)

**Abruzzo: Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, Colline Teramane, Pecorino**
- The regional DOC is now officially "ABRUZZO o D'ABRUZZO DOC". Pecorino needs "almeno il 90%" of the variety. Amended by DM 19 gennaio 2023. — [Consorzio Vini d'Abruzzo technical sheet (PDF)](https://www.vinidabruzzo.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ABRUZZO-o-DABRUZZO-DOC-sottozona-TERRE-DI-CHIETI.pdf)
- From the 2023 harvest, "Superiore" must be paired with one of four sub-zones: Colline Pescaresi, Colline Teramane, Terre de L'Aquila, Terre di Chieti. — [Wine Industry Advisor](https://wineindustryadvisor.com/2024/06/21/the-wines-of-abruzzo-are-superiori-the-revolution-of-the-abruzzo-model-begins-among-subzones-and-mentions-abruzzo-puts-quality-first/); [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2023/05/abruzzo-introduces-superiore-status/)
- The DOCG's official title is "Colline Teramane Montepulciano d'Abruzzo"; it requires at least 90% Montepulciano. — [AIS disciplinare (PDF)](https://aisitalia.it/Disciplinari/Abruzzo/DOCG/Colline%20Teramane%20Montepulciano%20d'Abruzzo.pdf). Its rules were re-registered under EU Implementing Regulation 2023/2171 (13 October 2023). Sources disagree on ageing: 24 months (12 in wood) is cited most often, with 36 months for Riserva. — [wein.plus glossary](https://glossary.wein.plus/montepulciano-d-abruzzo-colline-teramane); contradicted by [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/premium/discovering-montepulciano-dabruzzo-40-wines-tasted-and-rated-446578/)

**Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi**
- IMT quote: "The term 'Classico' is reserved for wines produced in the oldest region." — [Istituto Marchigiano di Tutela Vini](https://www.imtdoc.it/verdicchio/index_en.html)

- IMT: the DOC requires "at least 85% Verdicchio grapes, cultivated in 23 municipalities of the province of Ancona and in 2 of the province of Macerata". The Riserva became "Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva DOCG" in 2010. — [IMT](https://www.imtdoc.it/verdicchio/index_en.html)
- IMT terroir quote: the Esina valley "is affected, simultaneously, both by the fresh air from the mountains and the brackish winds from the Adriatic." — [IMT](https://www.imtdoc.it/verdicchio/index_en.html)
- IMT itself writes both "Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC Classico Superiore" and "…Classico Superiore DOC". — [IMT awards list (PDF)](https://imtdoc.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Vini-premiati-IMT-2025_Slow-wine.pdf)

**Irpinia and Campania**
- Consortium quote: Irpinia is "un territorio montuoso nel cuore della Campania… [con] suoli di matrice vulcanica, calcareo-argillosa e marnosa". — [Consorzio Tutela Vini d'Irpinia](https://consorziovinidirpinia.it/vini/)

## A2. Spain: Rioja DOCa and Granada

### Takeaway
Rioja has three zones (Rioja Alta, Rioja Alavesa, Rioja Oriental, the last renamed from Rioja Baja in 2018) and three origin tiers introduced in 2017 (Vino de Zona; Vino de Municipio, renamed "Vino de Pueblo" in 2024; Viñedo Singular). Its ageing terms are Genérico, Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva; a 2026 change dropped the 50-barrel minimum. For high-altitude Granada, use DOP Granada (also "D.O. Vinos de Granada") only when the label carries it.

### Cited Findings
- **Official ageing definitions (Consejo Regulador):**
  - Crianza: "a minimum two-year old ageing process and kept at least one year in a 225 litre… oak barrels".
  - Reserva: "3 years between oak barrel and bottle… at least 1 year in oak barrel plus a minimum… of 6 months in bottle".
  - Gran Reserva: "a 5-year minimum ageing process, 2 of them in… oak barrel plus 2 years in bottles".
  - White and rosé: Reserva 2 years and Gran Reserva 4 years, each with 6 months in barrel.
  - "Generic": "One-two year wines that keep main freshness and fruity characteristics".
  - — [Consejo Regulador DOCa Rioja, Classification](https://riojawine.com/en-gb/the-designation/classification/)
- **Official origin categories:** "'Vino de Municipio' classification grants the use of naming rights of the 144 Rioja municipalities"; "Viñedo Singular… was incorporated in 2017". (Page last modified 20 September 2024.) — [riojawine.com](https://riojawine.com/en-gb/the-designation/classification/)
- **2024 rename:** "the designation was changed from 'Vino de Municipio' to 'Vino de Pueblo'". Up to 15% may come from a neighbouring village; "viñedo en" may be used for 100% single-municipality wines. Reported 22 February 2024. — [wein.plus](https://magazine.wein.plus/news/rioja-introduces-new-designation-for-local-wines-vino-de-municipio-becomes-vino-de-pueblo)
- **Viñedo Singular requirements:** vines at least 35 years old, hand-harvested, reduced yields, vineyard controlled by the winery for at least 10 years; 148 registered by July 2023. — [Académie du Vin Library (secondary)](https://academieduvinlibrary.com/blogs/blog/rioja-new-regulations-five-years-on); [The Buyer](https://the-buyer.net/insight/riojas-terroir-revolution-vinedos-singulares)
- **2026 change:** on 24 July 2026 the plenary removed the minimum of 50 barrels needed to use Crianza, Reserva or Gran Reserva. — [Agronews Castilla y León](https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/rioja-elimina-minimo-50-barricas-vinos-crianza-reserva)
- **Zones, official quotes (Spanish):**
  - Rioja Alta: "En Rioja Alta predomina el clima atlántico con suaves temperaturas". — [riojawine.com Rioja Alta](https://riojawine.com/es/zona-de-produccion/rioja-alta/)
  - Rioja Alavesa: "El clima atlántico de Rioja Alavesa, situada más al norte, aporta más humedad a sus terruños y temperaturas generalmente más frías". — [riojawine.com Rioja Alavesa](https://riojawine.com/es/zona-de-produccion/rioja-alavesa/)
  - Rioja Oriental: "El influjo del clima mediterráneo es la clave de la zona más oriental." — [riojawine.com Rioja Oriental](https://riojawine.com/es/zona-de-produccion/rioja-oriental/)
- **Rioja Oriental** was formerly Rioja Baja, renamed in 2018 (one source says April 2018) because "Baja" implied inferiority. — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/premium/regional-profile-rioja-oriental-431356/); [Wine With Seth](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/rioja-oriental/)
- **Granada:** the Consejo Regulador uses "DOP Granada", "D.O. Granada" and "D.O. Vinos de Granada", covering "300 aprox Hectáreas". Official quote: vineyards "son de los más meridionales y a mayor altitud de toda Europa". — [Consejo Regulador DOP Granada](https://www.dopvinosdegranada.es/)
- **Altiplano de Sierra Nevada** is a separate IGP / Vino de la Tierra: granted in 2005 and renamed in 2009 from "Norte de Granada", which freed the name "Granada" for the quality wine. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano_de_Sierra_Nevada); [Junta de Andalucía pliego (PDF)](https://cdn.juntadeandalucia.es/export/drupaljda/VT_ALTIPLANO_SE_SIERRA_NEVADA.pdf)

### Inferences
- "DOCa Rioja", "Rioja DOCa" and "D.O.Ca. Rioja" are all correct; the Consejo's own branding is "DOCa Rioja".
- Write the zone as it appears on the label ("Rioja Alavesa"), and do not write a zone for a multi-zone blend.

### Gaps
- The Consejo's own text confirming "Vino de Pueblo" (its English page still read "Vino de Municipio" at its September 2024 revision); the date Granada was raised to DO (2018 vs a 2021 Ministry listing, per secondary sources).

## A3. Portugal: Vinho Verde, sub-regions, Loureiro and Avesso

### Takeaway
Use "Vinho Verde DOC" (the CVRVV writes "DOC Vinho Verde"). The nine sub-region names may be added to the DOC name; Lima is one of them, but Palmirinha is not in it.

### Cited Findings
- CVRVV quote: "The nine sub-regions—Amarante, Ave, Baião, Basto, Cávado, Lima, Monção and Melgaço, Paiva and Sousa—vary in terms of the maritime influence." — [CVRVV flyer (PDF)](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/fotos/documentacao/flyer_vinhos_verdes_eng_755552762686e8c83d0e70.pdf)
- CVRVV quote: "The soils are almost entirely granitic in origin, though layers of shale, clay, alluvium and even pebbles do exist." — [CVRVV flyer](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/fotos/documentacao/flyer_vinhos_verdes_eng_755552762686e8c83d0e70.pdf)
- CVRVV quote: "These valleys enable Atlantic breezes to push inland and influence the climate." — [CVRVV flyer](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/fotos/documentacao/flyer_vinhos_verdes_eng_755552762686e8c83d0e70.pdf)
- Labelling rule (Portuguese): "Podem ser utilizadas as denominações Sub-regionais… em complemento da denominação de origem". — [CVRVV labelling regulation (PDF)](https://portal.vinhoverde.pt/fotos/documentacao/regulamento_interno_da_rotulagem_produtos_com_denominacyayo_de_origem_do_vinho_verde__664998736916777d4abe1.pdf)
- Sousa covers Paços de Ferreira, Paredes, Lousada, Felgueiras, Penafiel and parishes of Vizela. — [WIPO Lex (Portuguese law)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/8108)
- A CVRVV grape page says one variety "is mainly cultivated in the Baião subregion" and has spread to Amarante and Paiva. Its Portuguese history page calls Baião and its neighbours "o berço desta variedade". — [CVRVV grape varieties (search excerpt)](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/vineyard/1-4-62/grape-varieties/); [CVRVV history](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/pt/regiao/historia-da-regiao/)

### Inferences
- The Baião excerpt almost certainly describes Avesso, but the excerpt does not name the grape.
- Loureiro is widely associated with Lima and Cávado. No official source was retrieved for that, so avoid tying it to a sub-region in copy.

### Gaps
- Official CVRVV wording for the Loureiro and Avesso heartlands.

## A4. France: Savoie, Bergerac, Côtes de Bourg, Bourgogne

### Takeaway
"Vin de Savoie" or "Savoie" AOP has 16 named crus (dénominations géographiques complémentaires); Cévins and Fréterive are not among them. The Bergerac–Duras interprofession now speaks of 17 AOPs. Côtes de Bourg has been an AOC since 1936. Burgundy has four levels across 84 appellations, including 33 Grands Crus. Meursault is a Village appellation with Premiers Crus.

### Cited Findings
**Savoie (INAO cahier des charges)**
- Name: "« Vin de Savoie » ou « Savoie »". The 16 crus are Abymes, Apremont, Arbin (red), Ayze, Chautagne, Chignin, Chignin-Bergeron (white), Crépy, Cruet, Jongieux, Marignan, Marin, Montmélian, Ripaille, Saint-Jean-de-la-Porte (red) and Saint-Jeoire-Prieuré. — [INAO (PDF)](https://extranet.inao.gouv.fr/fichier/PNOCDCVinSavoieouSavoie.pdf)
- INAO quote: "Le vignoble de Savoie s'égrène en arc de cercle sur les contreforts du massif alpin". — [INAO](https://extranet.inao.gouv.fr/fichier/PNOCDCVinSavoieouSavoie.pdf)
- INAO quote: vineyards "compris dans une gamme d'altitudes allant de 250 mètres à 500 mètres". — [INAO](https://extranet.inao.gouv.fr/fichier/PNOCDCVinSavoieouSavoie.pdf)
- Jacquère is about 50% of plantings; Persan is a rare red now recovering; Crémant de Savoie has been an AOC since 2014; Roussette de Savoie is for 100% Altesse. — [Wine Scholar Guild / Vinatis / Decanter (secondary)](https://www.winescholarguild.com/blog/regional-spotlight/the-heights-of-wine-discovering-savoies-alpine-offerings)

**Bergerac**
- IVBD quote: the vineyard spans "both banks of the River Dordogne and extending into the Lot-et-Garonne for the Duras area". It has "17 appellations available in five types"; the interprofession (IVBD) was formed by merger in 2014. — [Vins de Bergerac et Duras](https://www.vins-bergeracduras.fr/en/presentation-and-history/)

**Côtes de Bourg**
- Official quote: "Recognized as a Protected Designation of Origin (AOC in French) since 1936… located on the right bank of the Gironde Estuary and Dordogne River." It covers 3,400 ha across about fifteen communes. Reds are Merlot, Malbec, Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc; whites are Sémillon, Sauvignon Blanc, Colombard, Muscadelle and Sauvignon Gris. — [Côtes de Bourg official site](https://www.cotes-de-bourg.com/cdb/appellation/?lang=en)

**Bourgogne (BIVB)**
- BIVB quote: "Famous for its prestigious Climats, classified as Premier and Grand Crus, Bourgogne also offers some delightful Village and Régionale appellations." — [BIVB](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/)
- BIVB: "84 appellations", including "33 Grand Cru AOCs". — [BIVB Bourgogne Maps](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/our-resources/bourgogne-maps,3181,15261.html)
- BIVB definition: a Climat is "a plot of vines, gradually and precisely demarcated by man, and which has been recognized by its name for centuries"; "Climat refers specifically to a winegrowing plot where as lieu-dit is a generic geographical term." — [BIVB Climats](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/our-climats-and-lieux-dits/climats-and-lieux-dits-in-the-bourgogne/climats-and-lieux-dits-the-ultimate-expression-of-bourgogne-terroir,2461,9256.html?)
- Meursault is an "Appellation Village of the Côte de Beaune": 382.64 ha of whites, including 103.79 ha of Premier Cru. — [BIVB Meursault](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/bourgogne-and-its-appellations/meursault,2458,9253.html?&args=Y29tcF9pZD0yMjc4JmFjdGlvbj12aWV3RmljaGUmaWQ9MzQ5Jnw%3D)
- Labelling example: the appellation and its Premier Cru "may be followed by the name of the Climats of origin". — [BIVB Mercurey](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/bourgogne-and-its-appellations/mercurey,2458,9253.html?&args=Y29tcF9pZD0yMjc4JmFjdGlvbj12aWV3RmljaGUmaWQ9MzQ4Jnw%3D)

### Inferences
- AOC and AOP are both legally correct in French copy. Interprofessions increasingly write "AOP"; Côtes de Bourg itself writes "AOC".
- The BIVB writes "Bourgogne" rather than "Burgundy", and "Régionale / Village / Premier Cru / Grand Cru". Write "Meursault Premier Cru 'Les Charmes'" and "Bourgogne Côte d'Or 'Les Vaux'" (a regional appellation plus a lieu-dit).

### Gaps
- An official Vins de Savoie (interprofession) quote: no site found. An official Bergerac terroir quote beyond geography.

## A5. Germany: Pfalz, the 2021 wine law, VDP

### Takeaway
The 2021 law's origin pyramid has been binding from the 2026 vintage. "Großes Gewächs" and "Erstes Gewächs" are now terms regulated by law, but as of early 2026 the only GG and 1G wines on the market were the VDP's. Use VDP terms (GUTSWEIN, ORTSWEIN, ERSTE LAGE, GROSSE LAGE, GROSSES GEWÄCHS/GG) only for VDP members.

### Cited Findings
- DWI quote: "On 27 January 2021, the 10th Act amending the Wine Act came into force." — [Wines of Germany (Deutsches Weininstitut)](https://www.winesofgermany.com/wine-industry/wine-law-and-regulations)
- DWI quote: "The new regulations are binding from the 2026 vintage". — [Wines of Germany](https://www.winesofgermany.com/wine-industry/wine-law-and-regulations)
- DWI: the pyramid runs from "German wine" to PGI regional wines (Landwein) to PDO quality and Prädikat wines. Within PDO there are four levels: the whole wine region; defined areas or former Großlagen, which must now carry the word "Region"; a single municipality; and the single vineyard. — [Wines of Germany](https://www.winesofgermany.com/wine-industry/wine-law-and-regulations)
- DWI quote: "For the first time, the new German Wine Ordinance standardises and regulates the terms Großes Gewächs and Erstes Gewächs within single-vineyard wines." — [Wines of Germany](https://www.winesofgermany.com/wine-industry/wine-law-and-regulations)
- Under the new law, 1G yields are capped at 60 hl/ha (70 on steep slopes) with at least 11% ABV. "For now, the only 1G and GG wines on the market remain those produced under the VDP's existing classification system." (March 2026). — [SevenFifty Daily](https://daily.sevenfifty.com/a-guide-to-germanys-new-wine-classification-system/)
- VDP: a "four-level VDP classification pyramid adopted in 2012": VDP.GUTSWEIN, VDP.ORTSWEIN, VDP.ERSTE LAGE, VDP.GROSSE LAGE. — [VDP](https://www.vdp.de/en/kopf-navigation/service/presse/ajadam)
- VDP quotes: "VDP.GROSSE LAGE® is the designation for the highest quality German vineyards"; dry Grosse Lage wines "are called VDP.GROSSES GEWÄCHS® and can be recognised by the GG-logo"; "A dry VDP.ERSTE LAGE® wine is labelled as 'Qualitätswein trocken'". — [VDP Classification](https://www.vdp.de/en/the-wines/classification); [VDP flyer (PDF)](https://www.vdp.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Downloads/Broschueren/VDP-Classificationflyer-EN.pdf)
- Pfalz, DWI: "the chestnut trees on their wooded slopes attesting to the Palatinate's Mediterranean climate". About 40% of vines are red. — [Wines of Germany](https://www.winesofgermany.com/our-regions/winesight/231/villa-ludwigsh%C3%B6he); [deutscheweine.de](https://www.ch.deutscheweine.de/regionen/anbaugebiet/68/pfalz)
- The Pfalz has two Bereiche, Mittelhaardt-Deutsche Weinstraße and Südliche Weinstraße, and the Deutsche Weinstraße runs from Bockenheim to Schweigen. — [Wine With Seth (secondary)](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/pfalz/)

### Inferences
- Germany's legal spelling is "Großes Gewächs"; the VDP brands it "GROSSES GEWÄCHS" / "GG". Weingut Brand's wines should be described by village and site (for example "Bockenheim"), not with VDP tier names, unless Brand is a VDP member (not checked).

### Gaps
- A claim that the Bundesrat gave GG/1G a "new legal footing" on 12 June 2026 appears in one source only and is unconfirmed. — [Napa Valley Wine Academy](https://napavalleywineacademy.com/blogs/pouring-points/german-wine-classification-explained)

## A6. South Africa: Wine of Origin, wards, Old Vine Project

### Takeaway
The Wine of Origin hierarchy is geographical unit, region, district, ward. Stellenbosch is a district whose wards include Bottelary and Polkadraai Hills. Wellington has been a district since 2012, with five wards. The Old Vine Project's Certified Heritage Vineyards seal certifies vineyards of 35 years or older and shows the planting date.

### Cited Findings
- WOSA quote: "'W.O.' appears together with the name of a production area… it confirms that 100% of the grapes… comes from that specific demarcated area." — [WOSA, Wine of Origin scheme](https://www.wosa.co.za/The-Industry/Wines-Of-Origin/Wine-Of-Origin-scheme/)
- WOSA: a ward is "a relatively small demarcated viticultural area with a relatively homogeneous pattern of natural factors". The Coastal Region includes the districts Cape Town, Darling, Franschhoek, Lutzville Valley, Paarl, Stellenbosch, Swartland, Tulbagh and Wellington. — [WOSA](https://www.wosa.co.za/The-Industry/Wines-Of-Origin/Wine-Of-Origin-scheme/)
- WOSA: the Stellenbosch district's wards are "Banghoek, Bottelary, Devon Valley, Jonkershoek Valley, Papegaaiberg, Polkadraai Hills, Simonsberg-Stellenbosch and Vlottenburg". Wellington's five wards are "Blouvlei, Bovlei, Groenberg, Limietberg and Mid-Berg River". South Africa has "32 diverse districts and some 102 smaller wards". — [WOSA, Winelands](https://www.wosa.co.za/The-Industry/Winegrowing-Areas/Winelands-of-South-Africa/)
- Wellington was raised from a ward of Paarl to a district on 21 September 2012; its five wards were authorised in 2017. Vlottenburg was promulgated on 24 January 2020. — [Wine With Seth](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/wellington/); [Paarl Post](https://novanews.co.za/paarlpost/wellingtons-5-wine-wards-20171122-2/); [Wine With Seth, Vlottenburg](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/vlottenburg/)
- OVP quote: "In 2018 the Old Vine Project launched the Certified Heritage Vineyards seal that members can place on bottles of wine made from vineyards of 35 years or older, together with the planting date." — [Old Vine Project, About](https://www.oldvineproject.co.za/aboutus)
- OVP: the project "confirms the vineyard meets the 35-year minimum threshold, now the global standard set by the OIV in 2024". Vineyards "must be 35 years and older and registered with SAWIS". — [OVP seal page](https://www.oldvineproject.co.za/aboutus/certifiedheritagevineyardseal); [OVP membership](https://www.oldvineproject.co.za/becomeamember)
- Planting dates "can be verified by the South African Wine Industry Information Systems (SAWIS) back to 1900". The seal was launched to trade on 20 April 2018. — [Wines of South Africa UK](https://www.winesofsa.co.uk/news-features/world-first-as-a-new-certification-seal-is-launched-by-the-old-vine-project/)

### Inferences
- Write "Certified Heritage Vineyard(s)" exactly as on the seal. Give the planting year that is printed on it rather than "1960s".
- Write "WO Stellenbosch" or "WO Polkadraai Hills" as the label shows; "Bottelary Hills" is the estate's and route's informal usage, while the ward is "Bottelary".

### Gaps
- OVP blend threshold (85% from qualifying vineyards per one secondary source) not confirmed on the OVP site.
