## 1. How do top grower-focused importers structure producer pages? What facts lead, and in what order?

### Takeaway
Almost every grower-focused importer pairs a **scannable fact block** (people, place, hectares, soils/altitude, grapes, farming with certification status) with a **3–5 paragraph story** that runs place → people/history → vineyards → cellar → the wines' style, and ends there. The people's names and a hard number (hectares, altitude, vine age, founding year) show up in the first lines every time. Enotri's current pages have the story and gallery but are missing most of the fact fields buyers scan for: people, size, soils/altitude, grapes and certification.

### Cited Findings

**Exemplary sources (producer pages), each with what it leads with, its structure and a short quote**

1. **Louis/Dressner Selections: Cantina Giardino** (Enotri producer). The "Quick Facts" table comes first, in this order: Name of Estate, Region, Country, Proprietor, Size, Farming, Soils, Grapes grown, Fun facts. Then a dated "producer profile" (2 paragraphs, about 235 words), a long first-person visit report ("Words by Jules Dressner, photos by Eben Lillie"), dated harvest reports written by the producer (2014, 2020, 2023, 2024), and a list of about 30 cuvées. Farming is stated precisely as "Organic (Not Certified)". — [Louis/Dressner](https://louisdressner.com/producers/cantina%20giardino)
   - Quote: "From the beginning, the aim has been to source older vines (50+) from contadini who have been working traditionally for generations" — [Louis/Dressner](https://louisdressner.com/producers/cantina%20giardino)
   - Quote (Fun facts field): "The Giardino project was originally funded by the Esso gas station owned and operated by Antonio di Grutolla's brother." — [Louis/Dressner](https://louisdressner.com/producers/cantina%20giardino)
   - Cellar facts sit inside the profile: a varied set of vessels (oak and cherry-wood barrels, demi-muids, steel, fibreglass, home-made amphorae), longer élevage for the Aglianicos, skin contact for the whites, and sulfur only "in very rare cases" at bottling. — [Louis/Dressner](https://louisdressner.com/producers/cantina%20giardino)

### Inferences

#### Field order that recurs across importers (consensus)
1. People: proprietor/vigneron by name, and generation (Dressner, KL, Rosenthal, Polaner, Vine Trail, J&F all name people in the fact block or first paragraph).
2. Place: village, sub-zone, region, country.
3. Size: hectares, and often the number of parcels.
4. Terroir: soil and bedrock, altitude in metres, exposure, slope %.
5. Grapes grown.
6. Vine age (range or average).
7. Farming, with exact certification status (body and year), or "practising, not certified".
8. Cellar philosophy in one line (yeasts, vessels, SO2 approach).
9. Optional: founded / first estate bottling, annual production, "with us since", website, one signature fact.

The story then follows: place → people/history → vineyards → cellar → the wines' style, in 3–5 paragraphs of 235–380 words (Dressner, Tutto, KL). Long-form visit reports and harvest notes sit separately and carry dates (Dressner).

#### Critique of Enotri's current producer pages (Region, Farming, Wines in portfolio, story, gallery)
- **Missing people.** No sampled importer omits the vigneron's name. Add a "Vigneron / Family" field at the top. The current Ardoisières summary ("Terraced schist vineyards reclaimed from the alpine hillside…") never names Brice Omont.
- **"Region" is too coarse.** Importers give village plus sub-zone (KL: Montemarano within Taurasi; Vine Trail: Fréterive and Cévins). Split it into Location (village), Appellation(s) and Region/Country.
- **"Farming" needs certification precision.** Compare "Organic (Not Certified)" (Dressner), "Certified organic (Ecocert) practising biodynamic" (Vine Trail) and "Starting with vintage 2018, they are certified organic" (Vom Boden). Say whether it is certified, by whom, and since when.
- **Add Size (ha), Soils/Terroir (soil + altitude + exposure/slope), Grapes grown and Vine age.** These are the most consistent fields across the sample and the cheapest for a buyer to scan.
- **Replace adjectives with numbers.** "On impossible gradients" should become the figure Vine Trail gives ("slopes up to 60%"). "Reclaimed from the alpine hillside" is good: concrete and verifiable (Vine Trail documents the terrace restoration).
- **Keep the gallery, but caption it** (person, place, date). Rosenthal and J&F both run galleries; captions turn photos into evidence.
- **Add a "Facts verified [month year]" line**, given the hectare conflicts above.
- **Add downloads.** KL links tech-sheet PDFs per wine, Rosenthal offers "Download Label", Polaner has Print plus a soil-study PDF. Buyers and reps re-use these.
- **Add "With Enotri since [year]"** (Rosenthal's "Our first vintage together was 1978" is the model) and one signature fact (Dressner/J&F "Fun facts"). This is the place where the founder-led voice earns its keep.
- **Keep the CMS clean**, given the injected spam on the Vom Boden page.

#### Recommended template: Producer / estate profile page
```
[PRODUCER NAME]
[Village] · [Sub-zone / Appellation] · [Region], [Country]

Standfirst (museum label, 15–25 words; a fact, a place, a name, then stop):
  e.g. "[Name] farms [x] ha of [soil] at [altitude] m above [village]; [one defining fact]."

AT A GLANCE (in this order)
  Vigneron(s) / family ... [names; generation; year took over]
  Location ............... [village, sub-zone, region]
  Founded ................ [year; first estate bottling if different]
  Vineyards .............. [x ha in y parcels; named crus / lieux-dits / contrade]
  Soils & site ........... [soil over bedrock; altitude m; exposure; slope %]
  Grapes ................. [list, main first]
  Vine age ............... [range; average]
  Farming ................ [practice] + [certified by ___ since ___] OR "practising organic, not certified"
  Cellar ................. [yeast; main vessels; SO2 approach, one line]
  Production ............. [bottles or cases / year]
  With Enotri since ...... [year]
  Website ................ [url]
  Facts verified ......... [Month Year, with the producer]

STORY (3–5 short paragraphs, 200–400 words total; open each paragraph with a fact)
  1. Place: the landscape and why it matters (soil/altitude/climate), told with numbers.
  2. People: who, which generation, what they changed; one direct quote from the grower.
  3. Vineyards: parcels, crus, vine age, training, farming detail (cover crops, no herbicides…).
  4. Cellar: fermentation, vessels, élevage length, fining/filtration, SO2.
  5. The wines: one or two sentences on style in structural terms (acidity, tannin, weight), then stop.

ONE THING WE LOVE (optional, 1–2 sentences, founder voice): the signature detail.

WINES IN PORTFOLIO: card per wine (see Template 2), each linking to its tech sheet.
DOWNLOADS: tech sheets (PDF) · label images · hi-res photos · map.
GALLERY: captioned (person · place · year).
```

#### Things to avoid on producer pages
- Superlatives and unattributed rankings ("one of France's most exciting producers", "amazingly complex", "brilliantly unique"), as on [Martine's Wines](https://www.mwines.com/Producers/domaine-des-ardoisieres/) and [Polaner](https://www.polanerselections.com/producers/emidio-pepe/).
- Running down neighbours or the region to elevate the producer ("mass-produced, overly engineered versions", [Polaner](https://www.polanerselections.com/producers/emidio-pepe/)).
- Bare "organic"/"sustainable" with no certification status.
- Undated facts (hectares and parcel counts drift).
- Long scene-setting before the first fact. The KL page spends a full paragraph on the region before the estate's own hectares; that suits a consumer newsletter, but trade readers want the fact block first.
- Per-wine information reachable only through JavaScript tabs (Dressner); it is hard for buyers and search engines to reach.

### Gaps
- Skurnik, Bowler, Raeburn, Les Caves de Pyrene and Berry Bros. & Rudd producer pages were not retrieved (shared Firecrawl rate limit; WebFetch DNS failures). Their field order is not verified here.
- Only one or two producer pages per importer were read, so these are patterns, not a census.
- Kermit Lynch's dedicated grower page (kermitlynch.com/grower/terre-del-vescovo) was not scraped separately. The profile text came from the copy embedded on the wine page.

## 2. What does a professional tech sheet contain, and in what order?

### Takeaway
Real tech sheets fall into two camps. **Grower-importer sheets** (Kermit Lynch, J&F, Vine Trail) put the vineyard and cellar facts first and often have no analytical data. **Producer/brand sheets** (Reyneke, Vineyard Brands) lead with tasting notes and analysis (alc / pH / RS / TA). None of the sampled sheets has every field on Enotri's wish list. Closure, case production per wine, UPC/SKU, harvest dates, yields and training were rarely or never present, so Enotri would need to source those from producers directly.

### Cited Findings

**Exemplary sources (tech sheets)**

1. **Kermit Lynch wine page: Terre del Vescovo "Re'na Vota".** Order: "Viticulture / Vinification" bullets (fermentation yeast, vessel and duration; extraction method; élevage vessels with ages and sizes; time in botti then in bottle; the meaning of the cuvée name). Then a table with Blend | Vine Age | Soil | Vineyard Area. Then General Information (Wine Type, Country, Region, Producer, Winemaker, Farming). No tasting note and no analysis. — [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/wine/terre-del-vescovo/1520/TV03)
   - Quote: "Fermentation with indigenous yeasts in stainless steel tanks, 15-20 days" — [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/wine/terre-del-vescovo/1520/TV03)
   - Quote: "Aged in oak (2, 3, and 4 years old, and 225-L, 10-HL, and 50-HL)" — [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/wine/terre-del-vescovo/1520/TV03)
   - Vine age is given as a planting year ("Planted in 1952"). — [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/wine/terre-del-vescovo/1520/TV03)
2. **Kermit Lynch producer PDF: Vignai da Duline.** Per-wine table (Blend, Vine Age as planting years, Soil Type, Vineyard Area in ha), shared vinification bullets (hand-harvest, double sorting, natural fermentation, malolactic timing, vessel by colour) and per-wine élevage bullets with % new oak and time in bottle (e.g. "maximum 20% new oak"; "Aged in bottle for 3-4 months"). — [Kermit Lynch PDF](https://kermitlynch.com/files/vignai_da_duline-.pdf)
3. **Reyneke Organic Chenin Blanc 2025 (producer sheet; Enotri producer).** Order: wine name and vintage; main variety; **analysis line first**; then type, producer, style, winemaker, taste, wine of, body; tasting notes; ageing potential; blend; "in the vineyard"; "in the cellar"; contact details. — [Reyneke](https://www.reynekewines.co.za/wine_PDF.aspx?WINEID=51614)
   - Quote: "analysis alc: 12.5 | ph: 3.4 | rs: 2.0 | ta: 5.58" — [Reyneke](https://www.reynekewines.co.za/wine_PDF.aspx?WINEID=51614)
   - Quote: "The grapes are bunch sorted and destemmed and then undergo natural fermentation in stainless steel tanks." — [Reyneke](https://www.reynekewines.co.za/wine_PDF.aspx?WINEID=51614)
4. **Vineyard Brands (US importer) sheet: Essay Chenin Blanc 2023 (South Africa).** Order: positioning paragraph; Wine Description with COLOR / AROMA / PALATE / BODY lines; Vineyards; Winemaking; Technical Details (Varietal %, Grape Source, Residual Sugar, Total Acidity, pH); Vintage; Green Practices (IPW certified). The RS and TA figures carry no units, and there is no alcohol figure. — [Vineyard Brands](https://www.vineyardbrands.com/content/Tech%20Sheets/0096/0096-25-09-12-13-06-49-319.pdf)
   - Quote: "The unwooded wine was fined and filtered before bottling." — [Vineyard Brands](https://www.vineyardbrands.com/content/Tech%20Sheets/0096/0096-25-09-12-13-06-49-319.pdf)
5. **Jenny & François per-wine block (Costadilà).** Order: a quoted one-line tagline; Grapes; Vineyard (vine age or planting decade, place, soil); Making of (fermentation, maceration days, vessel, ageing time, filtration, SO2, refermentation method); Personality (winemaker's notes, ending with a food pairing). — [Jenny & François](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/costadila/)
   - Quote: "Bottled unfined, unfiltered, with no added SO2." — [Jenny & François](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/costadila/)
6. **Vine Trail per-wine block (Ardoisières).** Order: CUVÉE + vintage; appellation (Vin des Allobroges IGP); blend %; alcohol %; then one paragraph covering vine age, altitude, soil, yields (hl/ha), training ("sur échalas"), vessel and duration, SO2 in mg/l, tasting, and a drinking window ("For drinking now to 2030"). — [Vine Trail](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres)
   - Quote: "pinches of SO2 are added at racking and pre-bottling reaching 35mg/l total" — [Vine Trail](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres)
7. **Rosenthal per-wine page (DeForville Barbaresco).** Colour and grape tags, then a single paragraph (116 words): crus, average vine age (35 years), fermentation time and vessel, botti ageing of at least 18 months, then the tasting. "Download Label" is offered. — [Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/growers/deforville-83978/barbaresco-84529/)

**Secondary guidance (search summaries only, not read in full)**
- A tech sheet is a short document, usually about one page, that summarises a wine's significant features; there is no set format. — [Wine Insiders](https://wineinsiders.com/wine-101/what-is-a-wine-tech-sheet) (search summary only)
- Technical data does not define quality but helps compare vintages. Typical total acidity is roughly 4–8 g/l, and the sheet's readers are distributors, salespeople, restaurants, retailers and writers. — [Wine Folly](https://winefolly.com/deep-dive/understanding-wine-tech-sheets/) (search summary only)
- Tech sheets matter most for export, where samples cannot always be sent. — [Blarlo](https://blog.blarlo.com/en/how-to-write-a-wine-technical-sheet/) (search summary only)
- Winemaking should be written as specific decisions (vessel, temperature, malolactic or not, skin time, % new oak and origin), and production stated in bottles. — [EditFast](https://editfast.com/editing-services/agriculture-and-food/viticulture-and-wineries/) (search summary only; an editing-services page, low authority)

#### Recommended template: Tech sheet (one page, PDF and web)
Order: identity → place → farming → cellar → analysis → tasting → service → logistics. This follows the grower-importer order (vineyard before tasting) and adds the analysis and logistics blocks found on producer and brand sheets.
```
HEADER: Producer · Cuvée · Appellation (DOC/DOCG/AOP/DO/IGP) · Vintage · Colour/Style (still/sparkling; dry/off-dry)
        Enotri logo · "Facts from the producer, verified [Month Year]"

1 IDENTITY      Grape(s) & % · Country / Region / Appellation · Producer & winemaker (names)
2 VINEYARD      Site/parcel & village · Soil (topsoil/bedrock) · Altitude (m) · Exposure / slope %
                Vine age or planting year · Training (e.g. guyot, pergola, bush/échalas) · Density (vines/ha, optional)
                Yield (hl/ha) · Area (ha)
3 FARMING       Practice + certification body & year (or "practising, not certified")
4 HARVEST       Hand / machine · Date(s) · Sorting
5 CELLAR        Destemmed / whole-bunch % · Fermentation: vessel, yeast (indigenous/selected), temperature control, duration
                Maceration (days) · Malolactic (yes/no/partial)
                Élevage: vessel, size (L/hl), age or % new oak, duration; lees contact
                Fining / filtration · SO2 (total mg/L, or "added at bottling only")
6 ANALYSIS      Alcohol % vol · Residual sugar g/L · Total acidity g/L (state "as tartaric") · pH
7 TASTING       2–3 sentences in SAT order: nose → palate → structure (acidity, tannin, body, finish)
8 SERVICE       Drinking window · Serving temp (°C) · Decant? · By-the-glass: yes/no · One food pairing
9 LOGISTICS     Production (bottles/cases) · Bottle size(s) · Closure (natural cork/DIAM/screwcap/crown)
                Case configuration (6/12) · UPC/EAN · Alberta product code / SKU [confirm the code format with the Alberta trade research] · Importer: Enotri Wine Imports
```

#### Things to avoid on tech sheets
- Analytical figures without units, or no alcohol figure (Vineyard Brands sheet).
- "Aged in oak" with no size, age or % new. KL's "(2, 3, and 4 years old, and 225-L, 10-HL, and 50-HL)" is the standard to copy.
- Marketing paragraphs at the top (Vineyard Brands opens with a positioning blurb). Trade readers want the identity line, then the facts.
- Vintage-specific facts (harvest dates, analysis) inside evergreen producer copy. Keep them on the vintage sheet.
- "Sustainable" with no scheme named (KL's Farming field "Sustainable" on Terre del Vescovo). Name the scheme (e.g. IPW, as Vineyard Brands does).

### Gaps
- No sampled sheet listed closure, UPC/EAN or per-wine case production. Industry practice for these fields is not documented here; get them directly from each producer.
- Harvest dates, yields and training were rare. I did not find a published industry standard that sets tech-sheet field order; the order above is synthesised from the sample.
- Dressner per-wine tech fields were not captured (JavaScript-rendered).
- A Les Caves de Pyrene or Berry Bros. & Rudd sheet was not retrieved for a UK comparison.

## 3. What vocabulary do WSET SAT, the CMS grid and GuildSomm use, and which descriptors read as professional vs amateur? How do professionals describe structure concisely?

### Takeaway
Both WSET and the CMS describe structure on fixed five-step scales: low / medium(-) / medium / medium(+) / high for acidity, tannin, alcohol, body and finish. The CMS adds tannin texture and palate texture words, plus "mineral" and "earth" families. On a trade site, the professional move is one structural fact plus two concrete descriptors. Lists of fruit and adjectives like "gushing" read as amateur. Jancis Robinson's advice is to describe the wine's shape in the mouth rather than list flavours.

### Cited Findings

**WSET Level 3 SAT (Issue 2, 2022)**
- Scales: Acidity, Tannin and Body run low, medium(-), medium, medium(+), high (body ends at "full"); Finish runs short, medium(-), medium, medium(+), long; Sweetness runs dry, off-dry, medium-dry, medium-sweet, sweet, luscious; Intensity runs light to pronounced. — [WSET L3 SAT (hosted copy)](https://www.uoguelph.ca/hftm/system/files/2023%20wset_l3wines_sat_en_may2022_issue2.pdf)
- Quality scale: faulty, poor, acceptable, good, very good, outstanding. Readiness: "too young", "can drink now, but has potential for ageing", "drink now: not suitable for ageing", "too old". Development: youthful, developing, fully developed, tired. — [WSET L3 SAT](https://www.uoguelph.ca/hftm/system/files/2023%20wset_l3wines_sat_en_may2022_issue2.pdf)
- The lexicon is organised as primary (grape and fermentation), secondary (lees, malolactic, oak) and tertiary (oxidation, fruit development, bottle age). Its "Other" primary cluster is "flint, wet stones, wet wool"; red bottle-age terms include "forest floor", "earth", "savoury". — [WSET L3 SAT](https://www.uoguelph.ca/hftm/system/files/2023%20wset_l3wines_sat_en_may2022_issue2.pdf)

- WSET says the SAT may be reproduced only with written permission (usually free). — [WSET SAT page](https://wsetglobal.com/qualifications/25.asp) (search summary only). **Implication: quote SAT terms in copy, but don't reproduce the grid itself on the Enotri site.**

**Court of Master Sommeliers (Americas), Advanced/Master deductive format (2024)**
- Tannin is split into volume (Low to High) and texture: "Silky, Soft, Gritty, Coarse, Rough, Stalky, Hard". — [CMS-A 2024 grid](https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/2024-CMS-A-Advanced-and-Master-Sommelier-Deductive-Tasting-Format.pdf)
- Texture: "Lean, Linear, Crisp, Soft, Creamy, Round, Waxy, Oily, Viscous". Phenolic bitterness is noted for whites. — [CMS-A 2024 grid](https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/2024-CMS-A-Advanced-and-Master-Sommelier-Deductive-Tasting-Format.pdf)
- Mineral: "Mineral, Wet Stone, Limestone, Chalk, Slate, Flint". Earth: "Forest Floor, Mushrooms/Truffles, Potting Soil, Compost". Fruit condition ranges from Tart through Ripe, Fresh, Baked, Stewed, Dried, Jammy and Candied to Oxidized. — [CMS-A 2024 grid](https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/2024-CMS-A-Advanced-and-Master-Sommelier-Deductive-Tasting-Format.pdf)
- Sweetness: "Bone Dry, Dry, Dry with Slight Residual Sugar, Off-Dry…". Oak is assessed for presence, intensity and type (French, American, Eastern European). Climate is Cool, Moderate or Warm. — [CMS-A 2024 grid](https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/2024-CMS-A-Advanced-and-Master-Sommelier-Deductive-Tasting-Format.pdf)
- At Certified level, candidates tick generic descriptors, add one or two specific ones, and mark only the most expressive. — [CMS tasting grid instructions](https://courtofmastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/Tasting-grids-and-instructions.pdf) (search summary only)

**JancisRobinson.com: Tamlyn Currin, "Words matter – but which ones?" (13 Aug 2022)**
- Quote: "it is more important to describe the shape of the wine in your mouth than to list flavours" (Jancis's advice, as reported by Currin). — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/words-matter-which-ones)
- Quote: "‘Flowery’ or ‘florid’ or ‘gushing’ are favourite terms of derision for those who break the unwritten rules." — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/words-matter-which-ones)
- Currin says the SAT-style system is "a key communication tool with examiners, fellow students, lab technicians, wine buyers and producers", but dull for persuading the public ("Perhaps as enthralling as reading an invoice"). — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/words-matter-which-ones)
- A reader complaint she quotes: "why do the reviewers seem to need to put every fruit in their descriptions?" — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/words-matter-which-ones)

**Professional structure phrasing in the importer sample**

- "a bit of 'gras' on the attack and a lively finish with hints of quinine and lime" — [Vine Trail](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres)
- "the schist soils bring verticality and length to the wine" — [Vine Trail](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres)

**Consumer register, for contrast**
- A consumer glossary lists "rich, opulent, intense, structured, and muscular" for full-bodied wines and "smooth, velvety" for mouthfeel. — [WineCountry](https://www.winecountry.com/blog/common-wine-descriptions/)

### Inferences

#### Professional vocabulary bank for Enotri (trade-safe)
- **Acidity:** high / medium(+) / bright / taut / lifted / "high-toned" (KL); avoid "zingy" and "zesty" in trade copy.
- **Tannin:** say volume plus texture: fine-grained, silky, dusty, chalky, firm, grippy, stalky (CMS list plus KL/Rosenthal usage).
- **Body/texture:** light / medium(-) / medium / medium(+) / full; lean, linear, round, creamy, waxy, "gras" (CMS + Vine Trail).
- **Finish:** short to long; saline, bitter-almond, quinine; or use length plainly ("long").
- **Mineral/earth (CMS-sanctioned):** wet stone, flint, chalk, slate, forest floor. Pair them with the soil fact so they don't read as unearned (Vine Trail ties "verticality" to schist).
- **Development:** youthful, developing; "drink now–2030" (SAT readiness language, Vine Trail windows).

#### What reads as professional vs amateur on a trade site
| Reads professional | Reads amateur |
|---|---|
| One structure fact (acid/tannin/body) + 2 concrete descriptors | 5+ fruits in a row (reader complaint at JancisRobinson.com) |
| SAT/CMS scale words ("medium(+) acidity", "fine-grained tannins") | "Smooth", "easy-drinking", "bold", "delicious", "amazing" (consumer register) |
| Concrete referents: lemon pith, quinine, hot stone, almond | Abstract stacks: "elegant, complex, harmonious, balanced" |
| Mineral word tied to a stated soil | "Minerality" alone, with no soil or structure context |
| A drinking window | "Will age beautifully" |
| Full stop | Exclamation marks (Vine Trail's "high drinkability!" is the exception, not the rule) |

#### Concise structure formulas (from the sample)
- Acidity + texture: "taut acidity, waxy texture"
- Tannin volume + texture: "firm, fine-grained tannins"
- Weight + tension: "light-bodied but taut" (cf. Vine Trail's "weight and tension")
- Finish + one note: "long, saline finish"

### Gaps
- GuildSomm articles (often members-only) were not retrieved; only the CMS grid represents sommelier-exam vocabulary here.
- The WSET Level 4 SAT (which adds finer gradations) was not retrieved.
- No study was found that quantifies which descriptors trade buyers rate as "professional". The professional/amateur table is an inference from the standards and the importer sample.

## 4. How long are effective portfolio wine blurbs? Do professionals lead with place/farming or with flavour? What food and service notes do trade sites include?

### Takeaway
Grower-focused importers **lead with place, vines and cellar, and put flavour last**. Producer and brand sheets do the reverse. Short-form lines (8–16 words) exist as taglines or one-liners next to fuller per-wine notes of 46–116 words. Enotri's 16–17-word notes sit right in the tagline band, which suits the museum-label voice, but the fuller note and the data fields are missing. ### Inferences

#### Critique of Enotri's current wine note
"Organic, basalt-rich volcanic soils of the Roncà hills; crisp citrus, almond and a flinty savoury edge."
- **What works:** it leads with place and farming, as grower importers do (Vine Trail, Rosenthal, KL). It has concrete descriptors (almond, flint) and a full stop. The length (16 words) matches Vom Boden's one-liners and J&F's taglines.
- **What's missing or weak:**
  - (1) No grape or appellation. Fine on a card only if a field shows them; otherwise the sentence has to.
  - (2) "Organic" without a certification marker. Move it to a badge field with its status.
  - (3) Four flavour/feel words ("crisp", "citrus", "almond", "flinty savoury edge") and no structural fact. Under the shape-over-flavour advice, swap "crisp" for an acidity or texture term ("taut", "medium(+) acidity", "waxy").
  - (4) "Savoury edge" plus "flinty" doubles up. Pick one.
  - (5) No cellar fact, though one (vessel or lees) is often what separates two wines from the same estate (KL/Vine Trail practice).

#### Critique of the current producer summary
"Terraced schist vineyards reclaimed from the alpine hillside, planted to Jacquère, Mondeuse and Persan on impossible gradients."
- **Strong:** it starts concrete (terraces, schist, a reclamation story) and names the grapes.
- **Fixes:** add the name (Brice Omont) and a number ("up to 60% slopes", 400–500 m, per [Vine Trail](https://www.vinetrail.co.uk/growers/domaine-des-ardoisieres)), and drop "impossible". The museum-label rule ("a fact, a place, a name") currently has the fact and the place but no name.

#### Recommended template: Portfolio wine card + wine detail
```
CARD (portfolio grid)
  [Producer] — [Cuvée]
  [Appellation] · [Grape(s)]                     ← fields, not prose
  [Badge: Certified organic (body) / Practising organic / Biodynamic (Demeter) …]
  Blurb, 15–30 words, in this order; then stop:
    [Place: soil/altitude/site OR vine age] ; [one decisive cellar fact if any] ; [two concrete descriptors] + [one structure word].
  Trade line (small type): BTG ✓/✗ · Serve [x]°C · Pair: [one dish]      ← Enotri-specific addition; not seen in sample

DETAIL (wine page above the tech sheet), 50–90 words:
  Sentence 1: role in the range ("the estate's benchmark Soave", cf. Rosenthal "standard-bearer").
  Sentence 2: vineyard (vine age, soil, altitude, exposure).
  Sentence 3: cellar (fermentation, élevage vessel/duration, SO2 if notable).
  Sentence 4: nose/palate in SAT order with one structure statement.
  Sentence 5: drinking window + one specific pairing.
  [Download tech sheet] [Download label]
```
Worked pattern (bracketed items are placeholders to confirm with the producer, not facts):
- Card: "[Grape] from basalt-rich volcanic soils in the Roncà hills, [certified organic]. [Steel-fermented, x months on lees.] Citrus, almond, flint; taut acidity." (about 20–25 words)

#### Things to avoid in wine blurbs
- Fruit-salad lists of 5+ fruits ([JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/words-matter-which-ones) reader complaint). Rosenthal's six-item run "truffle, earth, berries, rose, tar and leather" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/growers/deforville-83978/barbaresco-84529/)) is the upper limit even for a 116-word note.
- Stacked adjectives ("dense, silky, fine-grained"; "deep, nuanced"). Pick one per noun, as the brand voice requires.
- Vintage-specific claims in evergreen card copy (Reyneke's note opens with "The 2025 vintage"). Fine on a vintage sheet, wrong on a perennial card.
- Exclamation marks and value talk on the card ("terrific value for money and high drinkability!", Vine Trail). Put value on the price list.
- Mineral words with no anchor. Anchor them to the stated soil.

### Gaps
- No trade survey or study found that sets an optimal blurb length; the ranges above are observed, not tested.
- Serving temperature and by-the-glass suitability did not appear on any sampled importer page. Recommending them is my own inference for Enotri's on-premise buyers, not documented practice.
- Punch and GuildSomm guidance on list or shelf-talker copy was not retrieved.

## 5. What do sommeliers and buyers say they want from an importer's producer and wine pages?
