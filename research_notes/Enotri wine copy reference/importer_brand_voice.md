# Importer and Merchant Brand Voice: A Register and Vocabulary Reference for Enotri Wine Imports

Research date: 15 October 2026. Method: Firecrawl scrapes and search excerpts of the importers' and merchants' own pages, plus trade press. WebFetch and direct curl were blocked by the local proxy, so every page read went through Firecrawl. Quotes are verbatim from the scraped page or a Firecrawl search excerpt and are kept to about 25 words or fewer. A few items come only from WebSearch summaries, not from the page itself. These are marked "(summary-level)" and should not be quoted word for word. All other wording is paraphrase.

Enotri's live copy was read from the repo for comparison: `Enotriwebsite/src/routes/index.tsx`, `about.tsx` and `components/site-chrome.tsx`.

## Q1. How do the most respected US grower-focused importers describe their mission and selection criteria?

### Takeaway
The most credible US importers set out their selection criteria as concrete rules or refusals. Examples: "farmers who own their land", "we have no brands", "if we won't drink it, we won't sell it", "only from the source". They back these with checkable facts: founding year, number of growers, time spent abroad, how the wine is shipped. Their mission statements are short. Proof comes through specifics and through what they say they will not do, not through adjectives. Where they slip into adjective strings (Polaner, Skurnik, De Maison), the copy reads like everyone else's.

### Cited Findings

**Kermit Lynch Wine Merchant (Berkeley, est. 1972)**
- Homepage carries no slogan. It shows the name and "est. 1972". — [kermitlynch.com](https://kermitlynch.com/)
- Selection criteria are stated as practice: "purchasing wines direct from the domaines and focusing on small producers whose wines express the authentic style of their native region." — [Our Company](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)
- Guarantee: "We only import wine we drink and enjoy ourselves, directly from the source." The same section lists only growers "from whom we buy every year." — [Our Company](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)
- Here "provenance" means chain of custody, not origin: refrigerated trucks and containers, and "we guarantee 100% perfect provenance with every bottle." — [Our Company](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)
- Audience is named plainly: "Whether you're a private client or a member of the trade". Sales channels are listed by type (retail shops, California and New York wholesale "serving retailers and restaurateurs", national distribution). — [Our Company](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)

**Louis/Dressner Selections (New York, founded 1988)**
- Footer blurb: "Louis/Dressner Selections is a portfolio of over 100 vignerons hailing from France, Italy, Germany, Portugal, Slovenia and Chile." It then names the partners. — [louisdressner.com](https://www.louisdressner.com/)
- Defines its grower model in plain words, as "working with farmers who own their land, grow their own grapes and make and sell their own wine" (the propriétaire/récoltant model). — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- The 1999 manifesto opens with refusals: "We have no brands. We are not looking for them." — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- Proof of fieldwork: "Collectively, we spend nearly nine months a year in Europe working with our growers". — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- Admits the criteria have changed: "We tend to be stricter in our criteria on farming and winemaking than we once were." — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- Names its audience: "a website written for wine professionals *attempting* to glean information on the wines we import". — [louisdressner.com](https://www.louisdressner.com/)

**Rosenthal Wine Merchant (founded late 1970s)**

- Also uses "sense of place" ("wines which vibrate with a sense of place and hum with real character") and "somewhereness". — [About](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/)

**Trade-press view of what makes an importer credible**
- A Wine Spectator column says a sommelier will look for other wines from the same importer after enjoying one, treating the importer on the back label as a selection standard. — [Wine Spectator, "Flip it over"](https://www.winespectator.com/articles/flip-it-over-15447) (summary-level)
- Meininger's: importers weigh quality, reputation, back-story and portfolio fit, and avoid producers that duplicate ones they already carry. — [Wine Business International](https://www.wine-business-international.com/wine/power-lists/what-are-us-importers-looking) (summary-level)
- The best importers spend real time in vineyards and cellars learning the process and the people. — [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/things-to-do/food-and-recipes/why-you-need-to-know-wine-importers/6AHYSZ5EV5CSZC22M7WEHYE4CM) (summary-level)

### Inferences
- The strongest selection criteria can be tested. "Farmers who own their land, grow their own grapes and make and sell their own wine" (Dressner) and "buy every year … directly from the source" (Kermit Lynch) can both be checked. Enotri's "we only take on estates that farm their own fruit" is in this register and is its best line of criteria.
- Top importers often skip a homepage slogan. They lead with growers, news or harvest reports (Dressner, Polaner, Kermit Lynch). Where there is an H1 (Rosenthal), it is one plain claim of 5 to 8 words followed by one proof sentence.
- Defining yourself by negatives is a hallmark of the best houses: Dressner's "no brands", Rosenthal's "not … marketability, nor … prestige", Wasserman's "no ideal type", Vom Boden's "less a matter of dogma". It shows judgment without praising yourself.
- Even respected houses lapse into prestige adjectives (Polaner, Skurnik, De Maison's redesign). Reputation carries those houses; a new agency cannot rely on it.

### Gaps
- "Domaine-bottled", "farmed by the family that bottles it", "typicity" and "allocation" did not appear in brand-level copy on any US importer site checked. They seem to belong to producer pages, tech sheets and trade offers rather than About pages. Kermit Lynch's "direct from the domaines" is the closest match found.
- Theise's current import affiliation in 2026 could not be confirmed. His catalogues are archived on terrytheise.com.
- Becky Wasserman-Hone's current status (a SevenFifty retrospective exists) was not verified in this session: [SevenFifty Daily](https://daily.sevenfifty.com/what-becky-wasserman-taught-us-about-burgundy-and-humanity/).

## Q2. How do established UK and European merchants phrase their about, heritage and curation copy?

### Takeaway
UK merchants split into two registers. The heritage houses (Berry Bros. & Rudd, Justerini & Brooks, Lay & Wheeler) open with a founding year, an address or a royal warrant, then a superlative that can be checked ("Britain's oldest"). The independent importers (Les Caves de Pyrene, Raeburn, Swig, Liberty) open with what they champion and back it with a number. The facts persuade; the adjectives ("multi-award-winning", "unrivalled", "amazing") are where they go generic.

### Cited Findings
- **Berry Bros. & Rudd:** "Founded in 1698, Berry Bros. & Rudd is Britain's oldest wine and spirits merchant. Family-owned and run, the company still trades from No.3 St James's Street". — [BBR History](https://www.bbr.com/about/history)
- **BBR (company change):** opened a Washington, DC store, a US website and a private-client service in November 2025. In the press release an executive uses luxury language ("luxury British brands and true craftsmanship"); the merchant's own pages lead with age and address. — [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/11/berry-bros-rudd-crosses-the-atlantic-with-us-launch/)
- **Justerini & Brooks:** "Founded in 1749, and granted the Royal Warrant by every consecutive British monarch since King George III in 1761". The history timeline lists named grower cities (Bordeaux, Cadiz, Reims, Dijon, Palermo). — [J&B Our History](https://www.justerinis.com/about-us/our-history)
- **Lay & Wheeler:** "Lay & Wheeler was founded in 1854, and remains one of Britain's longest standing fine wine merchants." It "works with over 600 wine producers". — [laywheeler.com](https://www.laywheeler.com/). Its history page relies on abstract nouns ("a story of innovation, ambition and integrity"). — [Our History](https://www.laywheeler.com/about-us/our-history)
- **Lay & Wheeler (company change):** Naked Wines sold it to Coterie Limited for £11.3m (2019). — [Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/naked-wines-sells-fine-wine-merchant-lay-wheeler-11-3m-14m-425599/); [Harpers](https://harpers.co.uk/news/fullstory.php/aid/25983/Naked_Wines_sells_Lay___Wheeler_to_Coterie_for__A311.3m.html)
- **Les Caves de Pyrene:** "an importer, agent, distributor and retailer of wines from around the world." "We champion small, independent winemakers, the hardy souls who put their love and life's work into the bottle". — [lescaves.co.uk](https://www.lescaves.co.uk/lescaves-home)
- **Raeburn Fine Wines (Edinburgh):** "Raeburn is a family owned wine importer and distributor. We are Edinburgh based, with a London office since 2010." — [Raeburn tariff PDF](https://www.raeburnfinewines.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Raeburn-Wines-Web-Tariff-Aug-2022.pdf). Proof line: "99.9% of our stock is shipped from the growers by us". — [Raeburn list PDF](https://www.raeburnfinewines.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Raeburn-Wines-2022-Birthday-and-Anniversary-wine-list-WEB.pdf)
- **Raeburn origin (third party):** "Zubair Mohamed started selling wine at the back of his father's florist shop in Edinburgh in 1984." — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/a-taste-of-raeburn)
- **Lea & Sandeman:** credibility is carried by a third-party quote on its press page: "skilful at nosing out producers nobody else has". — [L&S Press](https://www.leaandsandeman.co.uk/content/press.html). Jancis Robinson: an "excellent reputation for real quality of wine and service." — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/lea-sandemans-current-treasures). Co-founder Patrick Sandeman has died; WSJ ran a tribute. — [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB10000872396390444813104578016300571376958)
- **Liberty Wines:** origin told as a modest fact: "Starting with a staff of just four, David Gleave MW founded Liberty Wines in 1997". — [BFT directory](https://www.thebft.co.uk/index.php/business-directory/item/liberty-wines). Its LinkedIn blurb uses puffery: "multi-award-winning … unrivalled customer service". — [LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/company/liberty-wines-ltd). A director gives scale as a count: "over 2,000 wines from 400 producers". — [The Buyer](https://www.the-buyer.net/people/supplier/iwsc-top-distributors-tom-platt-on-taking-liberty-wines-to-the-next-level)
- **Swig (London, strong in South Africa):** "Swig has been championing independent growers since 1997. We work with family-owned producers" and names its Organic Food Federation certification. The same page uses "we are for wine lovers not wine snobs" and "amazing wines made by amazing people in amazing places". — [Swig Meet the Team](https://swig.co.uk/pages/our-story)

### Inferences
- The heritage-house formula is year + place + verifiable superlative ("oldest", "Royal Warrant since 1761"). A start-up cannot borrow it, but the method transfers: put the founding year and the city where the business trades in the footer and About lede.
- The independents prove credibility with ratios and counts (99.9% shipped by us; 400 producers; since 1997) and with named certifications, not with descriptions of themselves.
- Swig is a useful negative example for Enotri. It shares Enotri's focus on independent growers and South Africa, but its "amazing ×3" line is exactly the stacked-adjective pattern Enotri's guideline forbids.

### Gaps
- The Liberty Wines and Raeburn About pages were not scraped in full (rate limits). Raeburn's lines come from its own PDFs and a search excerpt.
- No current (2026) ownership change was found for J&B, Les Caves or Raeburn. This was not exhaustively checked.

## Q3. How do Canadian wine agencies, especially in Alberta, Ontario and BC, present themselves to the trade, and whom do they say they serve?

### Takeaway
Canadian agency copy is mostly route-to-market and channel language: liquor boards, licensees, consignment, "all channels". It uses more corporate superlatives ("leading", "largest", "most prestigious premium") than the US grower-importers do. The boutique and natural-wine agencies (Le Sommelier, The Living Vine, Garneau Block, Juice Imports) lean on category labels and counts. Almost none tell a specific grower or founder fact on the homepage. That leaves a real gap for a grower-led, founder-led Alberta agency that writes in the US-importer register.

### Inferences
- Canadian agency vocabulary for audiences: licensees, on-premise, retail, liquor boards/monopolies, private retailers, consignment (Ontario), restaurateurs, private clients, wine collectors. In Alberta, "licensee" is the regulatory word, and private clients buy through licensed retailers, not from the agency. Any Enotri claim to sell to "private clients" should reflect that. The exact wording is for the Alberta legal-copy researcher to confirm.
- Superlatives are the norm in Canadian corporate agency copy: largest, leading, most prestigious, revolution, innovative solutions. Short, factual, grower-named copy would stand out in Alberta.
- "Sense of place" is already Le Sommelier's line in Ontario, and Trialto has registered "People, Place & Time". So "place" alone does not set an agency apart in Canada.
- Garneau Block owns "natural / low-intervention / family-run" in Edmonton. Enotri's clearest difference is the farm-own-fruit criterion, the founder's cellar visits and the six-country spread, not a farming-method label.

### Gaps
- No full About pages were scraped for Crush Imports, Liquid Art Selections or the Alberta natural-wine agencies (rate limits). Their positioning beyond the homepage line is unknown.
- No complete public list of Alberta agencies was found.
- The date of the Trialto/Liquid Art merger was not captured.

## Q4. How do founder-led boutique importers write the founder story credibly?

### Takeaway
Credible founder stories are built from small, odd, checkable facts: a year, a sum of money, a number of cases, a first wine, a former job, a named mentor. Two voices work. Third person suits a founder who is now an institution (Kermit Lynch, Rosenthal, Wasserman). First person suits a founder still writing the copy (Vom Boden, Swig, Dressner's son). Vanity is avoided through self-deprecation, through naming other people, and by putting the growers or the work at the centre.

### Cited Findings
- **Kermit Lynch (third person, wry):** "In 1972, with a $5,000 loan and a bit of gumption, this writer/musician opened Kermit Lynch Wine Merchant with thirty-five cases of wine…". Honours are stated flatly: two James Beard awards and the Légion d'honneur. Close friends are named (Richard Olney, Alice Waters). — [About Kermit](https://kermitlynch.com/about-kermit)
- **Kermit Lynch (humour against vanity):** raised where "they served Welch's grape juice in communion at his dad's church". — [About Kermit](https://kermitlynch.com/about-kermit)
- **Louis/Dressner (first person, written by the founders' son):** "a company started by two people who knew absolutely nothing about wine or its commerce." The page calls itself "An updated, perhaps slightly fictional introduction to who we are." — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- **Louis/Dressner (the turning-point wine):** the story pivots on a specific 1987 discovery at Domaine de Roally and the question "Why is this so different?". — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- **Rosenthal (first wine and first shop):** "Since our very beginnings as a one-man retail operation on the Upper East Side of Manhattan". — [About](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/). "the very first wine Neal ever purchased for import was the legendary Carema from the Ferrando family". — [rosenthalwinemerchant.com](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/)
- **De Maison (first wine as founding text):** the Catalan saying from the label of "the first wine we imported, Avinyó Cava". — [demaisonselections.com](https://demaisonselections.com/)
- **Becky Wasserman (biography facts):** "an American expatriate who has lived in Burgundy since 1968". — [About](https://www.beckywasserman.com/about-us/). SevenFifty adds that she was "selling wine barrels before she sold wine". — [SevenFifty Daily](https://daily.sevenfifty.com/what-becky-wasserman-taught-us-about-burgundy-and-humanity/)
- **Jenny & François (credentials told as a story):** "Jenny Lefcourt, a native New Yorker, originally landed in Paris to study French film during her PhD at Harvard." — [About](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/about-us/)
- **Vom Boden (first person, claim with a qualifier):** "I do not think there is an import book with a larger number of smaller estates. Yes, we are the biggest of the smallest." — [About](https://www.vomboden.com/about/)
- **Swig (first person, method not ego):** "My mentor got me tasting wines blind, which leads you to think about the taste, not the label". — [Swig](https://swig.co.uk/pages/our-story)
- **Raeburn (origin told by a third party):** started "at the back of his father's florist shop in Edinburgh in 1984." — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/a-taste-of-raeburn)

### Inferences
- **Facts credible founder stories include:** year founded, the city, what the founder did before (journalism student, film PhD, musician), the first wine or first grower, a tiny starting number ($5,000, 35 cases, staff of four), a mentor or friend by name, and a present-day working fact (how many visits a year, who tastes).
- **What they leave out:** words about the founder's palate, "passion", "vision" or "expertise"; lists of credentials without context; claims about "the finest".
- **Voice:** third person reads as institutional and modest when the founder is the brand. First person reads as honest when one founder runs everything. Mixing the two on one page (Enotri's About moves from "we" to "Rawlings…He") is common and acceptable if each section stays consistent.
- **Enotri's founder section** ("Rawlings founded Enotri Wine Imports to bring wines of genuine provenance to Alberta… He works directly with the growers…") has no founding year, no prior career, no first estate and no number. Against this benchmark it reads as a placeholder.

### Gaps
- No founder-led Canadian agency with an exemplary founder story was found in the time available. Canadian sites mostly list staff without biographical facts.

## Q5. Which phrases have become trade clichés that a discerning sommelier would roll their eyes at?

### Takeaway
The cliché list is well documented. The worst offence is stringing several clichés into one "wine speak" sentence. Credible houses avoid them, or use them once and explain them ("terroir" with an actual soil or site).

### Cited Findings
- Sean P. Sullivan's list of nine words "the industry has beaten to death": unique, terroir, world class, finest, carefully, premium, ideal, boutique, hand-crafted. — [Northwest Wine Report, 11 June 2025](https://www.northwestwinereport.com/2025/06/nine-words-the-wine-industry-has-beaten-to-death.html)
- Sullivan on "unique": "Unless you say *what makes* something unique, then you really aren't saying anything". — [Northwest Wine Report](https://www.northwestwinereport.com/2025/06/nine-words-the-wine-industry-has-beaten-to-death.html)
- On "boutique": combining "small" and "boutique" is "at best, redundant". — [Northwest Wine Report](https://www.northwestwinereport.com/2025/06/nine-words-the-wine-industry-has-beaten-to-death.html)
- On stacking: "stringing together a bunch of wine industry terms and buzzwords that don't really say anything" is "wine speak". — [Northwest Wine Report](https://www.northwestwinereport.com/2025/06/nine-words-the-wine-industry-has-beaten-to-death.html)
- An older buzzword list names terroir, artisan, handcrafted and reserve; commenters added "Terroir Driven" and "Family Owned". — [Fermentation blog, 2005](https://fermentationwineblog.com/2005/09/buzzjargon_wine/) (summary-level)
- Matt Kramer says words like "elegant" are overused to the point of meaninglessness but still defends keeping core terms. — [Wine Spectator](https://www.winespectator.com/articles/matt-kramer-3-essential-wine-words) (summary-level)
- A New York sommelier calls "natural" a word without a fixed definition. — [StarChefs](https://www.starchefs.com/features/buzzed-words) (summary-level)
- Vom Boden calls out its own phrase as "the clichéd 'less is more' thing". — [Vom Boden](https://www.vomboden.com/about/)

**Lapses found on otherwise-respected sites (pattern examples, not to imitate):**
- Triple adjective: "amazing wines made by amazing people in amazing places". — [Swig](https://swig.co.uk/pages/our-story)
- Gem and gorgeous language, plus a value triad: "beautiful gems", "gorgeous wines", "Integrity / Passion / Commitment". — [Polaner](https://www.polanerselections.com/about/)
- Superlative scope: "the finest portfolio of wines available at every price point". — [Skurnik](https://www.skurnik.com/portfolio-wine/)
- Abstract poetry: "Wines of integrity, stories with soul"; "merchants of culture, connection, and craft". — [De Maison](https://demaisonselections.com/)
- Unprovable service claims: "multi-award-winning … unrivalled customer service". — [Liberty Wines LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/company/liberty-wines-ltd)
- Corporate filler: "innovative solutions and outstanding service". — [Empson Canada](https://www.empsoncanada.com/). "secret ingredient", "most prestigious premium wineries". — [Trialto](https://trialto.com/)
- Movement language: "LEADING THE WINE REVOLUTION". — [SBSW](https://sbswwineimports.com/). "Discover the true essence of artisanal wine!" — [Vino Al Vino](https://www.vinoalvino.ca/)

### Inferences
- **Eye-roll list for Enotri's purposes** (from the sources above plus the patterns observed):
  - Sullivan's nine: unique, terroir used without a site, world-class, finest, carefully, premium, ideal, boutique, hand-crafted.
  - Seen on the sites checked: passion/passionate, gems, curated or curated selection, authentic/authenticity as a bare claim, "true essence", "wine lovers not snobs", "punch above their weight", "from soil to glass/cellar", "stories with soul", "exceptional value", "renowned and emerging regions", value triads (Integrity/Passion/Commitment), "revolution".
  - Also "luxury" or "prestige" framing, which appears in Canadian corporate copy and press quotes, not on grower-importer pages.
- **"Passion" specifically:** it appears on Polaner, Trialto, Renaissance, Select and Swig staff copy, and on none of the grower-importer mission statements (Dressner, Rosenthal, Vom Boden, Wasserman, Kermit Lynch). That absence is the signal.
- **"Sense of place"** is not a cliché in itself; Rosenthal and Skurnik use it. It is generic as a headline because Le Sommelier, Skurnik, Rosenthal and Trialto (as "Place") all use it. It works only when tied to a named site.

### Gaps
- No trade-press source was found that names "passion" as a cliché; the passion point above is an inference from usage patterns.
- No Canadian trade-press piece on agency copy clichés was found.

## Q6. Component reference: hero/positioning, about/philosophy, founder/story, footer/"who we are"

### Takeaway
Each component has a typical length, shape and way of proving credibility. All four follow one rule, which matches Enotri's museum-label guideline: a fact, a place, a name, then stop. The detail below lists exemplary sources, vocabulary, structure and what to avoid for each.

### Cited Findings

#### 6A. Hero / positioning line
(a) Exemplary sources:
- Rosenthal H1: "We're devoted to small, family-owned estates", plus one proof sentence. — [rosenthalwinemerchant.com](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/)
- Kermit Lynch: name plus "est. 1972"; no slogan. — [kermitlynch.com](https://kermitlynch.com/)
- Jenny & François "What We Do": "to defend an endangered species — winemakers who work like artisans". — [jennyandfrancois.com](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/)
- Zev Rovine: "Purveyor of Natural Wine, Artisanal Spirits & Pure Sake", then a "began in 2007" fact. — [zrswines.com](https://www.zrswines.com/)
- Bowler: "Most are family farms run by owner/winemakers." — [bowlerwine.com](https://www.bowlerwine.com/)
- BBR meta title: "Britain's oldest fine wine & spirits merchant". — [bbr.com](https://www.bbr.com/)
- Garneau Block (local benchmark): category H1 "NATURAL, BIODYNAMIC, AND LOW-INTERVENTION WINES". — [garneaublock.com](https://www.garneaublock.com/)
- Negative example: De Maison's three stacked headings ("A Curated World…", "Wines of integrity, stories with soul"). — [demaisonselections.com](https://demaisonselections.com/)

#### 6B. About / philosophy
(a) Exemplary sources:
- Becky Wasserman: three short paragraphs covering founding fact, a refusal ("We do not endorse any single method of vinification"), and a house rule ("If we won't drink it, we won't sell it."). — [About](https://www.beckywasserman.com/about-us/)
- Rosenthal: sections titled "The Art of Discovery", "Our History", "Our Staff", "Our Distributors", each about 80 to 120 words. — [About](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/)
- Louis/Dressner: a long narrative followed by a principles list (Wild Yeasts, Hand Harvesting, Low Yields, Non-Filtration…). — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)
- Vom Boden: an anti-glamour opener ("logistics *suck*"), scale commitment, farming stance, link to grower pages. — [About](https://www.vomboden.com/about/)
- Kermit Lynch "Our Guarantee": buying rule, logistics proof, then who is served. — [Our Company](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)
- Bowler: two paragraphs: what the company is, then "emphasis … on the people behind the product". — [About](https://www.bowlerwine.com/about)

#### 6C. Founder / story
(a) Exemplary sources: Kermit Lynch ($5,000, 35 cases, 1972) — [About Kermit](https://kermitlynch.com/about-kermit); Louis/Dressner ("knew absolutely nothing about wine") — [About](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us); Rosenthal (one-man shop; first wine, Ferrando Carema) — [About](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/); Becky Wasserman ("in Burgundy since 1968") — [About](https://www.beckywasserman.com/about-us/); Jenny Lefcourt (film PhD to Paris wine bars) — [About](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/about-us/); Swig's Robin Davis (blind tasting with a mentor) — [Swig](https://swig.co.uk/pages/our-story); Raeburn (father's florist shop, 1984) — [JancisRobinson.com](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/a-taste-of-raeburn).

#### 6D. Footer / "who we are" blurb
(a) Exemplary sources:
- Louis/Dressner: "a portfolio of over 100 vignerons hailing from France, Italy, Germany, Portugal, Slovenia and Chile. We are a partnership of Denyse Louis…". The blurb then names the other two partners. — [louisdressner.com](https://www.louisdressner.com/)
- Raeburn: "a family owned wine importer and distributor. We are Edinburgh based, with a London office since 2010." — [Raeburn PDF](https://www.raeburnfinewines.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Raeburn-Wines-Web-Tariff-Aug-2022.pdf)
- Kermit Lynch: "KERMIT LYNCH WINE MERCHANT est. 1972". — [kermitlynch.com](https://kermitlynch.com/)
- Bowler: "an independent, family-owned fine wine and spirits distributor and importer based in New York City". — [About](https://www.bowlerwine.com/about)
- Les Caves de Pyrene: "an importer, agent, distributor and retailer of wines from around the world." — [lescaves.co.uk](https://www.lescaves.co.uk/lescaves-home)
- Le Sommelier: "Importers of fine wines into Ontario, Canada, focusing on wines with a sense of place." — [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/lesommelierwine/?hl=en)

### Inferences

**(b) Recurring professional vocabulary (observed in use; reliability notes in brackets)**
- **People:**
  - grower; small grower; grower-producer [De Maison]; vigneron(s) [Dressner]; winegrower(s) [Kermit Lynch]
  - farmers [Dressner]; family farms run by owner/winemakers [Bowler]; owner-winemaker
  - propriétaire/récoltant, defined as farmers who own their land, grow, make and sell their own wine [Dressner]
  - multi-generational, family-run [Rosenthal]; family-owned [Bowler, Swig, Raeburn]; human-scaled, small [Vom Boden]; independent [Swig, Les Caves, Bowler]
- **Relationship verbs:** represent [Vom Boden, Le Sommelier, Rosenthal]; champion [Swig, Les Caves, Vom Boden]; work with / work closely with [Le Sommelier, Zev Rovine]; buy every year [Kermit Lynch]; direct from the domaines / directly from the source [Kermit Lynch]; long-term relationships [Kermit Lynch, Zev Rovine].
- **Portfolio nouns:** portfolio [everywhere]; selection(s) [in many company names]; import book [Vom Boden]; back label [Rosenthal, Skurnik, Polaner]; growers/producers pages.
- **Place:** terroir, best paired with a concrete site [Rosenthal, Kermit Lynch, Becky Wasserman]; origins ("speaks clearly of its origins") [Rosenthal]; native region [Kermit Lynch]; sense of place [Rosenthal, Skurnik, Le Sommelier]; somewhereness [Rosenthal; term popularised by Matt Kramer]; rooted in place [Zev Rovine].
- **Provenance:** in fine-wine trade use this means chain of custody and storage: refrigerated trucks and containers, "100% perfect provenance" [Kermit Lynch], "shipped from the growers by us" [Raeburn]. It is less often used to mean origin character.
- **Farming and cellar:** organic, biodynamic, regenerative [Polaner]; low/minimal intervention [Bowler, Zev Rovine, Les Caves]; wild/native yeasts, hand harvesting, low yields, non-filtration [Dressner principles]; works its own vineyards.
- **Audience:**
  - US: the trade; private client [Kermit Lynch]; retailers and restaurateurs [Kermit Lynch]; restaurants and wine shops [Rosenthal]; distributors.
  - Canada: licensees, on-premise, retail [SBSW]; liquor boards/monopolies [Trialto, Whitehall]; consignment [Lifford]; private clients [Nicholas Pearce]; households and wine collectors [Select].
- **Observed rarely or not at all at brand level:** typicity, domaine-bottled, allocation. These belong to producer and tech pages or trade offers.

**(c) Structural patterns**
- **Hero:**
  - Length: none to about 8 words, often with one proof sentence of 20 to 35 words (Rosenthal).
  - Opening: the strongest are declarative claims about who they work with ("devoted to small, family-owned estates"), category labels (Zev Rovine, Garneau Block), or simply name plus year (Kermit Lynch).
  - Several top importers have no hero line at all; the homepage is the growers and the news.
- **About/philosophy:**
  - Length: 150 to 400 words in 3 to 5 short sections (Wasserman, Rosenthal, Bowler). Long-form manifestos (Dressner) are the exception, and they earn their length with humour and specifics.
  - Sentence shape: declarative, often built on contrast ("not X, but Y"; "less a matter of X … more a matter of Y"), often with a negation.
  - Sequence: founding fact, then criteria (often as refusals), then practice and proof, then people or staff, then who they serve and where.
- **Founder/story:**
  - Length: 120 to 250 words for the profile; the long story sits on its own page.
  - Opening: a biographical oddity or origin place, not a value statement.
  - Credibility: dates, small numbers, a named first wine or grower, named mentors, plainly stated awards.
- **Footer blurb:**
  - Length: 1 to 2 sentences, 15 to 35 words.
  - Formula: [what we are: importer/agent/distributor] + [scale: count of growers] + [origins: named countries] + [base: city, year] + optionally [ownership or partners].
- **How credibility is proved, by type:**
  - Years: est. 1972, 1979, 1988, 1996, 1997, 1698, 1749, 1854.
  - Counts: "over 100 vignerons", "more than 150 producers", "over 600 wine producers", "400 producers", "45 states", "fourteen people".
  - Time on the ground: "nine months a year in Europe".
  - Logistics: refrigerated containers; "99.9% … shipped from the growers by us".
  - Buying rules: "we drink and enjoy ourselves"; "won't drink it, won't sell it".
  - Scale caps: "it will stay that way"; largest grower under 40 acres.
  - Third-party praise: James Beard, Food & Wine Importer of the Year, a Jancis Robinson quote, all stated flatly or quoted rather than adjectivised.

**(d) What credible houses avoid**
- Stacked adjectives and value triads. The lapses are listed in Q5.
- Unprovable superlatives ("finest", "leading", "largest selection", "unrivalled", "most prestigious").
- Luxury and prestige framing on their own pages: BBR's own copy leads with age and address, and "luxury" appears only in a press quote.
- Ideological purity claims without nuance. Wasserman and Vom Boden explicitly refuse dogma; Dressner admits its criteria tightened over time.
- Description in place of evidence ("carefully", "meticulous", "dedicated", "passionate").
- Telling the reader how they will feel ("foretells a great wine drinking experience" is a Polaner lapse).

### Gaps
- Trade-deck "who we are" slides are not public, so footer blurbs and LinkedIn/WineAlign profiles stand in for them.

## Q7. What distinct positioning archetypes appear, and what is one example of each?

### Takeaway
Five main archetypes recur, plus two secondary ones. Enotri's current copy mixes the advocate and editor archetypes. The founder's verified cellar visits and the farm-own-fruit rule fit the advocate archetype best, and that is where its credibility is strongest.

### Cited Findings
1. **The importer as the growers' advocate.** Example: Vom Boden, "being the grower's champion, advocate and friend". — [Vom Boden](https://www.vomboden.com/about/). Related: Bowler, "emphasis has always been on the people behind the product". — [Bowler](https://www.bowlerwine.com/about)
2. **The importer as principled purist / manifesto-holder.** Example: the Louis/Dressner 1999 manifesto, "We have no brands." — [Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us). Related: Jenny & François defending "an endangered species". — [J&F](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/). The Theise Manifesto. — [Theise PDF](https://www.terrytheise.com/_files/ugd/d8e337_ae3e589aefbf45b49f4bbea33281f80d.pdf?index=true)
3. **The importer as discoverer and market-maker (aesthete-editor).** Example: Rosenthal, "not with following the market, but with creating the market", guided by "our own aesthetic North Star". — [Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/). Related: Polaner "turning over stones". — [Polaner](https://www.polanerselections.com/about/)
4. **The importer as guarantor of provenance (custodian-logistician).** Example: Kermit Lynch, "100% perfect provenance with every bottle". — [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company). Related: Raeburn, "99.9% of our stock is shipped from the growers by us". — [Raeburn PDF](https://www.raeburnfinewines.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Raeburn-Wines-2022-Birthday-and-Anniversary-wine-list-WEB.pdf)
5. **The merchant as historian and institution.** Example: Berry Bros. & Rudd, "Founded in 1698 … still trades from No.3 St James's Street". — [BBR](https://www.bbr.com/about/history). Related: Justerini & Brooks and the Royal Warrant since 1761. — [J&B](https://www.justerinis.com/about-us/our-history)
6. *(Secondary)* **The merchant as convivial guide / anti-snob.** Swig, "we are for wine lovers not wine snobs". — [Swig](https://swig.co.uk/pages/our-story)
7. *(Secondary, common in Canada)* **The agency as route-to-market service partner.** Trialto, "helping liquor boards, retailers and restaurants source, market and sell". — [Trialto](https://trialto.com/about/). Related: Select's "servicing all channels". — [Select](https://www.selectwines.ca/)

### Inferences
- The historian archetype is closed to a young agency. The guarantor archetype is open only if Enotri can name its shipping and storage practice.
- **Recommendation: build on the advocate archetype.** Use the editor archetype only as a light second note ("short on purpose"). It fits a founder-led agency that visits every estate, and it is under-used in Alberta, where competitors use the category-label and route-to-market archetypes.

### Gaps
- Not tested: how Alberta buyers (sommeliers, retailers) actually react to each archetype. This would need interviews.

## Q8. How does Enotri's current copy compare with best practice, and which phrases read as generic or clichéd?

### Takeaway
Enotri's body copy on criteria is already near best practice: estates that farm their own fruit, someone has walked the rows before listing, short on purpose, a price an Alberta list can carry. Its headline and mission lines are the weakest parts. "Good wine has a sense of place", "true provenance", "vineyard stewards", "authenticity, quality, and harmony" and "boutique" are the most generic phrases in the trade, or are used in a way trade readers will read differently. The founder section lacks the hard facts that credible founder stories rely on.

### Cited Findings (benchmarks the assessment relies on)
- "Sense of place" is used as a positioning line by Le Sommelier ("focusing on wines with a sense of place") and appears at Skurnik and Rosenthal. — [Le Sommelier Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/lesommelierwine/?hl=en); [Skurnik](https://www.skurnik.com/portfolio-wine/); [Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/)
- Trialto has registered "WINES OF PEOPLE, PLACE & TIME®" and operates in Calgary. — [Trialto](https://trialto.com/)
- "Rooted" and "roots" phrasing is common: "rooted in place" — [Zev Rovine](https://www.zrswines.com/); "Rooted in tradition, driven by terroir" — [De Maison](https://demaisonselections.com/); "rooted in their land" — [De Maison](https://demaisonselections.com/)
- Fine-wine trade use of "provenance" means chain of custody. — [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company); [Raeburn PDF](https://www.raeburnfinewines.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Raeburn-Wines-2022-Birthday-and-Anniversary-wine-list-WEB.pdf)
- "Boutique" is on Sullivan's list of overused words, and Nicholas Pearce uses "Boutique, hand-crafted". — [Northwest Wine Report](https://www.northwestwinereport.com/2025/06/nine-words-the-wine-industry-has-beaten-to-death.html); [npwines.com](https://www.npwines.com/)
- "Exceptional value" is Lifford's line: "wines of exceptional value from dedicated producers". — [WineAlign](https://www.winealign.com/wine_agents/975)
- Value triads (Polaner's "Integrity / Passion / Commitment") are a recognisable pattern. — [Polaner](https://www.polanerselections.com/about/)
- A concrete farming criterion ("farmers who own their land, grow their own grapes and make and sell their own wine") is the benchmark for Enotri's "farm their own fruit". — [Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)

## Q9. Which coined or trademarked phrases must Enotri not reuse?

### Takeaway
Several competitors own signature phrases, and one is a registered mark. Trialto's "Wines of People, Place & Time®" carries the highest risk: it is registered, and Trialto operates in Calgary in Enotri's own market. Enotri should avoid any "people / place / time" triad.
