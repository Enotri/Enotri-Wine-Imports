Enotri Wine Imports Inc — Edmonton, Alberta

Boutique, terroir-driven wine, imported into Alberta and put in front of restaurants, wine shops, and hotels — one grower relationship at a time. This is the working reference for how Enotri looks, sounds, and holds itself across the website, price lists, and event material.

01

Brand essence

Enotri is the ground before the label — the grower, the soil, and the hands that made the wine, told before the tasting note. The site and every piece that extends it should feel walked-through, not scrolled past.

02

Voice & tone

Enotri writes like a museum label, not a wine-list blurb: a fact, a place, a name — and then it stops. Confidence comes from specificity, never from adjectives stacked on top of each other.

Write like this

Nebbiolo, Langhe. Hand-harvested, October.

Fourth generation on the same hillside.

Chosen for the ground it came from.

Not like this

Indulge in this exquisite, show-stopping red!

A luxurious explosion of bold flavor.

The perfect wine for every occasion.

03

Wordmark

Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the height of the wordmark's cap-height on every side. Nothing else — copy, photography, chrome — enters that margin.

Minimum size

96px wide on screen, 1 inch in print. Below that, the "ROOTS MATTER" line stops reading and should be dropped, wordmark only.

Ground

The mark is warm paper on cellar charcoal by default. It only ever appears on dark, low-contrast grounds — never on white, never on a busy photograph without a scrim.

✕

Recolor to gold, umber, or brand color outside warm paper / white

✕

Stretch, skew, or reset the type to a different serif

✕

Add a drop shadow, outline, or glow effect

✕

Place directly on unmasked photography

04

Color

Cellar Charcoal
#0D0D0C

Primary ground for every surface. Near-black, never flat — warmed by the gradients that sit on top of it.

Panel
#191917

Raised surfaces: cards, form fields, the browser-mockup chrome.

Panel Deep
#20201D

A second step up for nested panels, sparingly.

Warm Paper
#F0E8DC

Primary ink. Headlines, the wordmark, and anything that needs to read first.

Muted Taupe
#A49B8F

Body copy and captions. Quieter than Warm Paper on purpose — it should never compete with a headline.

Harvest Gold
#D2A16D

The one accent. Eyebrows, active states, the terroir trace, underlines on links — never a fill, never a large area.

Estate Umber
#A85C43

Atmosphere — hero gradients, glow, the browser chrome dots. Warmth in the background, rarely in text.

Cream
#EEE2D1

The one filled surface: the primary button. Reserve it for the single most important action on a page.

Hairline
rgba(232,220,204,.16)

Every border and divider. Structure, not decoration — it should be felt before it's noticed.

Gold owns a region, not the page. If a layout starts to feel busy, the fix is almost always fewer gold elements, not a second accent colour.

Typography

Weight 400, optical size tuned to scale. Carries every headline, chapter title, and pull-quote. Tight tracking, tighter leading.

06

Imagery

What it is

Documentary, natural light, human-scaled. A hand in soil, a working cellar, a real table — never a styled flat-lay.

Grade

Sun-warmed and slightly desaturated — never a glossy, oversaturated "wine marketing" grade. Skin tones and stone stay true.

Sequence

Land → growers → fruit & process → estate → invitation. The camera moves like a visitor would, on foot, chapter by chapter.

07

Signature motifs

Rolling hills silhouette

A low horizon line closes the hero and the footer — vineyard topography standing in for a generic wave or gradient fade. It is the one recurring shape in the system; it does not appear anywhere else.

Langhe Bordeaux Rioja Estate

The terroir trace

A fixed contour line runs along the bottom of the viewport. As a visitor passes each chapter, the line draws that section and stamps the region name into the ledger — part progress indicator, part index of the growers' places. Reserve it for the chaptered, long-form site; it is not a generic UI element to drop into shorter pages.

Portfolio browser

The wine list appears as a mock desktop window — chrome dots, a sidebar of filters, a grid of bottles — instead of a plain product grid. It signals "this is the whole portfolio, browsable," inside an otherwise editorial page.

Interface

Primary button
Pill radius, Cream fill, DM Sans 600, lifts 2px on hover.
Text link
Warm Paper text, Harvest Gold underline — never a colour change on hover, only the lift.
Gutter
clamp(1.25rem, 5vw, 5.5rem) — the page margin at every width.
Borders
1px Hairline only. No card ever gets a shadow and a border together.
Motion
Reveals and gentle parallax only. `prefers-reduced-motion` drops every transform and keeps opacity — content stays reachable, never hidden.
09

Do & don't

Do

  • Name the place and the person before the tasting note.
  • Let a photograph run full-bleed and quiet — one caption, not three.
  • Use gold for one thing per screen: a label, a link, a stamp on the trace.
  • Cut hard between chapters. A seam is allowed to show.

Don't

  • Stack adjectives where a fact would do more work.
  • Introduce a second accent colour to "add energy."
  • Round every corner and shadow every card — spend those by role.
  • Blend chapters into one continuous parallax world; that effect belongs to a different kind of page.
