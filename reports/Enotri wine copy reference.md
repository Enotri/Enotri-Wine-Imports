# Name the Grower, Give the Number, Stop

The world's most respected wine importers earn trust with facts a reader can check and with things they say they will not do. Adjectives do not do that work. Kermit Lynch opened "with a $5,000 loan and a bit of gumption" and "thirty-five cases of wine" ([Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/about-kermit)). Louis/Dressner says "We have no brands" ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)). Rosenthal writes "Our first vintage together was 1978" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/growers/deforville-83978/)). Enotri's best lines already work this way: "Start with the grower. The wine follows.", "we only take on estates that farm their own fruit" and "short on purpose". Its headlines and mission lines do not. "Good wine has a sense of place", "true provenance", "authenticity, quality, and harmony" and "boutique" are the same generic words Enotri's Canadian competitors use. Accuracy is a bigger problem than tone. Only 4 of the 17 producers checked had copy with no errors. Eight of the nine certification claims on the sustainability page needed rewriting; they are now corrected on a branch that is waiting for the founder's approval. Five of the six Alberta ordering and tasting statements overstate or blur how AGLC and Connect Logistics work. Those errors are a legal exposure as well as a credibility one:

- **AGLC** treats a website as advertising that must be "accurate and verifiable" ([AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf)).
- **CFIA** treats a "certified organic" claim as misleading unless it names the certification body ([CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/organic-claims)).
- **The Competition Act** requires environmental claims to be backed by evidence before they are made ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/environmental-claims-and-greenwashing)).

The founder has since confirmed that Quinta da Palmirinha, Domaine des Ardoisières and Domaine Rougeot are not in the portfolio. They are being removed from the site. That leaves **14 estates in five countries: South Africa, France, Italy, Spain and Germany**. Portugal is no longer a sourcing country. The fix is the same for every page:

- a block of facts before the story;
- a name and a number in every opening line;
- the certifier named wherever "organic" appears;
- the trade process described the way AGLC defines it.

Some facts only the founder can supply: the founding year, his path before Enotri, the first estate he signed, how often he visits, and each producer's current certificates.

## Quoted passages are reference material, never site copy

**Quotes from other businesses in this report are for reference only.** Do not put them, or close paraphrases of them, on the Enotri site. Copying them would infringe the authors' copyright. It would also hurt Enotri's search rankings, because search engines treat duplicated text as unoriginal and rank the original page above the copy. Take the patterns, structures and vocabulary, and write your own sentences. The original copy offered in each component section below was written for this purpose. Louis/Dressner asks anyone reusing its writing to "PLEASE credit us when doing so" ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/)). ## Credible houses prove every claim with a number or a refusal

The research found five main types of importer positioning, plus two minor ones:

- the growers' advocate (Vom Boden is "the grower's champion, advocate and friend");
- the principled purist (Dressner's "We have no brands");
- the discoverer who creates a market (Rosenthal is "concerned not with following the market, but with creating the market");
- the guarantor of provenance (Kermit Lynch's "100% perfect provenance with every bottle");
- the institution with history (Berry Bros. & Rudd, "Founded in 1698");
- *(minor)* the friendly, anti-snob guide (Swig);
- *(minor, common in Canada)* the route-to-market service partner (Trialto, Select).

([Vom Boden](https://www.vomboden.com/about/); [Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us); [Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/); [Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company); [BBR](https://www.bbr.com/about/history)).

A young agency cannot use the history archetype. The guarantor archetype only works if Enotri can describe how it ships and stores wine. **Enotri should build on the advocate archetype, with a light editor note ("short on purpose").** It fits a founder who visits every estate, and nobody uses it in Alberta. Alberta competitors describe themselves by category ("a natural wine liquor agency in Alberta") or by the service they provide ("LEADING THE WINE REVOLUTION") ([Garneau Block](https://www.garneaublock.com/); [SBSW](https://sbswwineimports.com/)). Canadian agency copy also leans on corporate superlatives: "leading", "largest", "most prestigious premium wineries" ([Trialto](https://trialto.com/); [Empson Canada](https://www.empsoncanada.com/)). Short, factual copy that names growers would stand out in that market.

Credible copy relies on a small set of proofs. Each one has an Enotri equivalent the founder can supply:

| Proof type | How the best houses use it | Enotri equivalent to gather |
|---|---|---|
| Year | "est. 1972" ([Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/)) | est. [year] |
| Count | "over 100 vignerons" ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/)) | [14] estates, five countries |
| Time on the ground | "nearly nine months a year in Europe" ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)) | [number] cellar visits in [year] |
| Logistics ratio | "99.9% of our stock is shipped from the growers by us" ([Raeburn](https://www.raeburnfinewines.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Raeburn-Wines-2022-Birthday-and-Anniversary-wine-list-WEB.pdf)) | how wine travels to the CLS warehouse [if known] |
| Relationship date | "Our first vintage together was 1978" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/growers/deforville-83978/)) | "With Enotri since [year]" on each producer page |
| Scale cap | "it will stay that way" ([Vom Boden](https://www.vomboden.com/about/)) | "short on purpose" (already on site) |

Three cautions apply across all the components.

- **Avoid stock words.** Sean P. Sullivan lists nine words "the industry has beaten to death": unique, terroir, world class, finest, carefully, premium, ideal, boutique, hand-crafted. He calls "small" plus "boutique" "at best, redundant" ([Northwest Wine Report](https://www.northwestwinereport.com/2025/06/nine-words-the-wine-industry-has-beaten-to-death.html)). "Passion" appears on Polaner, Trialto, Renaissance, Select and Swig pages. It appears in none of the grower-importer mission statements (Dressner, Rosenthal, Vom Boden, Wasserman, Kermit Lynch). That absence is a signal. It is an observed pattern, not a published rule.
- **"Provenance" means something specific in the fine-wine trade: chain of custody and storage.** Kermit Lynch uses it for refrigerated containers, Raeburn for shipping ([Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)). Enotri's "true provenance" will read to trade buyers as a storage claim, not as a statement about origin.
- **Buyers want the story and the logistics together.** A GuildSomm educator asks, "Does it come from a producer who is telling a compelling story of a grape, place, or perspective?" The same piece's headline advice is "Make it easy to say yes" ([Vinexposium](https://www.vinexposium.com/en/actus/academy/buyers-unfiltered-what-it-actually-takes-to-get-on-the-list-in-2025/)). Each page therefore needs two layers: a museum-label line and story for the reader, and a fact block with a downloadable sheet for the buyer.

Enotri's own guideline already sets the target: "Enotri writes like a museum label, not a wine-list blurb: a fact, a place, a name — and then it stops." Its model line is "Nebbiolo, Langhe. Hand-harvested, October." (Enotri brand guidelines, `references/brand-guidelines.html`).

## Brand pages: make the grower line the headline and add three hard facts

### Homepage hero: the strongest line is two scrolls down

**What the best houses do.**
- Rosenthal's homepage H1 is one plain claim, "We're devoted to small, family-owned estates", followed by one sentence about "multi-generational, family-run enterprises" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/)).
- Kermit Lynch has no slogan, only the name and "est. 1972" ([Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/)).
- Bowler says who the growers are: "Most are family farms run by owner/winemakers." ([Bowler](https://www.bowlerwine.com/)).
- Jenny & François frame their mission as protection: "to defend an endangered species — winemakers who work like artisans" ([Jenny & François](https://www.jennyandfrancois.com/)).
- Garneau Block, the nearest local comparison in Edmonton, leads with a category, "a natural wine liquor agency in Alberta" ([Garneau Block](https://www.garneaublock.com/)). That means "natural / low-intervention" is already taken in Enotri's city.
- The negative example is De Maison's three stacked headings, ending "Wines of integrity, stories with soul" ([De Maison](https://demaisonselections.com/)).

The pattern is a headline of zero to eight words, then one proof sentence of 20 to 35 words. Some importers skip the headline and lead with growers and news instead.

**Word bank and things to avoid.**

| Use | Avoid (why) |
|---|---|
| grower, family estate, owner-winemaker | "sense of place" as a headline (Le Sommelier, Skurnik and Rosenthal all use it; Trialto's registered tagline includes "Place") |
| farm their own fruit; visited; tasted with the grower; before it is listed | "thoughtful selection", "curated" (curation cliché) |
| [count] estates; South Africa, France, Italy, Spain and Germany | "renowned and emerging regions"; "Europe" in place of named countries |
| Edmonton, Alberta; est. [year] | "boutique" (on Sullivan's overused-words list) |
| restaurants, bars, hotels and liquor stores | "Find your next favourite" (consumer register); "legendary" |
| | "Roots Matter" / "rooted" repeated in headlines (keep it on the logo only) |

**Structure.**
```
EYEBROW   Wine agency · Edmonton, Alberta · est. [year]
H1        5–8 words: who you work with, or the grower rule
PROOF     20–35 words: [count] estates · five named countries · one practice fact
CTAs      Meet the growers · See the wines · Trade enquiries
```

**Verdict on the current copy.**
- **H1 "Good wine has a sense of place." Rework.** Every competitor would agree with it, and it names no grower, place or fact.
- **"Start with the grower. The wine follows." Promote to H1.** It sits two scrolls down and is the strongest line on the site. No competing use of it was found.
- **Eyebrow "Independent wine imports · Edmonton, Alberta". Adjust the word "independent".** The shape is right, but "independent" does not hold for Zaccagnini, which has been 80% owned by Argea since 2023 ([Zaccagnini press kit](https://www.cantinazaccagnini.it/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/04/Zaccagnini-PK_2025_ENG_LR.pdf)). "Imports" is acceptable shorthand, even though AGLC is the legal importer of record.
- **"Each estate is chosen for its sense of place, its care in the vineyard and the people willing to stand behind every bottle." Retire.** It is a three-part list of virtues that proves nothing.
- **"A thoughtful selection, rooted in real places." Rework.** "Thoughtful selection" is the curation cliché, and "real places" implies that other wines come from fake ones.
- **"Every bottle has a reason to be here." Keep.**
- **"Family estates across Europe and South Africa". Fix.** Name the five countries, and allow for the Zaccagnini ownership point.
- **"Find your next favourite". Replace.** It is consumer language.
- **Meta title "Wines with roots" and description "thoughtfully chosen wines". Replace.** Both lean on clichés.

**Ready-to-use Enotri copy.**

> **Eyebrow:** Wine agency · Edmonton, Alberta · est. [year]
> **H1:** Start with the grower. The wine follows.
> **Proof line:** [Fourteen] estates in South Africa, France, Italy, Spain and Germany. Each one visited, and tasted with the grower, before a bottle is listed.

> Basalt in Roncà. Clay over limestone in Bourg. Granite above False Bay. The growers who farm them, brought to Alberta.

> *(Replacing "A thoughtful selection, rooted in real places")* From the basalt of Monte Calvarina to Pasetti's vineyards at 550 metres in Pescosansonesco, every bottle has a reason to be here.

> **Meta title:** Enotri Wine Imports — Grower wines for Alberta
> **Meta description:** An Edmonton wine agency representing [14] grower estates from South Africa, France, Italy, Spain and Germany, for Alberta's restaurants, bars, hotels and liquor stores.

### About and philosophy: the philosophy paragraph is close to best practice; the "simple goal" paragraph is not

**What the best houses do.**
- Kermit Lynch states its buying rule as a practice: "purchasing wines direct from the domaines and focusing on small producers whose wines express the authentic style of their native region." ([Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/our-company)).
- Dressner defines its growers in words a reader can test, "working with farmers who own their land, grow their own grapes and make and sell their own wine". It opens its manifesto with a refusal ("We have no brands. We are not looking for them.") and admits "We tend to be stricter in our criteria on farming and winemaking than we once were." ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)).
- Rosenthal defines itself by what does not guide it: "guided not by concerns about marketability, nor by questions of prestige, nor by ideological strictures, but by our own aesthetic North Star" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/about/)).
- Wasserman refuses any single doctrine: "We do not endorse any single method of vinification or an 'ideal' type of wine." ([Becky Wasserman](https://www.beckywasserman.com/about-us/)).
- Vom Boden puts a cap on its own size: "Vom Boden is a small company, focusing on small growers, and it will stay that way." ([Vom Boden](https://www.vomboden.com/about/)).

These pages run 150 to 400 words in three to five short sections. The order is: founding fact, then buying criteria (often stated as refusals), then practice and proof, then people, then who the company serves.

**Word bank and things to avoid.**

| Use | Avoid (why) |
|---|---|
| represent; work with; direct from the estate | "true provenance" (redundant; the trade hears chain of custody) |
| farm their own fruit; walked the rows; tasted in the cellar | "vineyard stewards" (a euphemism; the trade says growers, vignerons, farmers) |
| short on purpose; a price an Alberta list can carry | "authenticity, quality, and harmony" (abstract three-part list, the pattern of Polaner's "Integrity / Passion / Commitment") |
| "We do not …" (a stated refusal) | "renowned and emerging regions"; "exceptional value" (Lifford's stock line) |
| licensees: restaurants, bars, hotels, liquor stores | "boutique"; "passion"; "dedication" (cannot be checked) |

**Structure.**
```
LEDE          Who, where, when: Enotri Wine Imports is a wine agency in Edmonton, founded by [founder] in [year].
CRITERIA      The rule, stated so it can be checked; one refusal ("We do not …").
PRACTICE      How the rule is enforced: visits, tastings, [number] per year.
SCALE         Short on purpose: [count] estates; what that gives the buyer (an answer, a price).
WHO WE SERVE  Licensees by type; private buyers through liquor stores.
```

**Verdict on the current copy.**
- **Philosophy paragraph: keep, it is near best practice.**
  - "We only take on estates that farm their own fruit" is a rule a reader can test, in Dressner's style.
  - "Before a wine is listed, someone here has walked those rows and tasted in that cellar with the person responsible for it" uses the practice itself as proof.
  - "Short on purpose" and "a price that an Alberta list can actually carry" make the editor's case without boasting.
- **"Simple goal" paragraph: replace.** It is the weakest copy on the site. It packs "true provenance", "vineyard stewards", "authenticity, quality, and harmony", "renowned and emerging regions" and "exceptional value" into four sentences.
- **Lede "a boutique wine agency based in Edmonton, Alberta": add facts.** It has no year and no founder.
- **"A real answer rather than a marketing line": tighten.** It sounds slightly defensive; the best houses show the answer rather than promise one.
- **"Farm their own fruit": check before using it on the homepage.** Dressner says Cantina Giardino's aim has been "to source older vines (50+) from contadini" ([Louis/Dressner](https://louisdressner.com/producers/cantina%20giardino)). Confirm that Giardino farms or leases those vineyards rather than buying grapes. Confirm the same for every estate.

**Ready-to-use Enotri copy.**

> Enotri Wine Imports is a wine agency in Edmonton, founded by Rawlings Munemo in [year]. We represent [fourteen] estates in South Africa, France, Italy, Spain and Germany.

> We only take on estates that farm their own fruit [confirm for every estate]. Before a wine is listed, someone from Enotri has walked its rows and tasted in its cellar with the person who made it. We do not follow one method: some of our growers are certified organic or biodynamic, some farm organically without a certificate, and we say which, estate by estate.

> The list stays short on purpose. When a buyer asks why a wine tastes the way it does, the answer is a soil, a cellar and a name, and the price is one an Alberta list can carry.

> We work with Alberta's licensed restaurants, bars, hotels and liquor stores. Private buyers find our wines through Alberta liquor stores, and we will tell you which ones.

### Founder: the only hard fact about Rawlings is on the sustainability page

**What the best houses do.**
- Kermit Lynch writes in the third person, with wit and small numbers: "In 1972, with a $5,000 loan and a bit of gumption, this writer/musician opened Kermit Lynch Wine Merchant with thirty-five cases of wine…" ([Kermit Lynch](https://kermitlynch.com/about-kermit)).
- Dressner uses self-deprecation: "a company started by two people who knew absolutely nothing about wine or its commerce." ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/about-us)).
- Rosenthal anchors the story to a first wine: "the very first wine Neal ever purchased for import was the legendary Carema from the Ferrando family" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthalwinemerchant.com/)).
- Wasserman needs one biographical fact: "an American expatriate who has lived in Burgundy since 1968" ([Becky Wasserman](https://www.beckywasserman.com/about-us/)).
- Swig's founder credits a method rather than himself: "My mentor got me tasting wines blind, which leads you to think about the taste, not the label" ([Swig](https://swig.co.uk/pages/our-story)).
- Vom Boden qualifies its own claim with humour: "Yes, we are the biggest of the smallest." ([Vom Boden](https://www.vomboden.com/about/)).

Credible founder profiles run 120 to 250 words. They include a year, a city, a former life, a first wine or grower, a small starting number, a named mentor and a working fact about today. They leave out the founder's palate, "passion", "vision" and "expertise".

**Word bank and things to avoid.**

| Use | Avoid (why) |
|---|---|
| founded in [year]; before that, [job] | passion, vision, palate, expertise |
| first estate signed; first shipment; [number] cases | "wines of genuine provenance" (soft, and repeats "provenance") |
| worked in South African vineyards and cellars (already on the site) | "bottles that carry the mark of …" (says nothing checkable) |
| answers the trade enquiries himself (already on the site) | lists of credentials with no context; "the finest" |
| a named mentor or grower | switching voice within one section |

**Structure.**
```
OPENING   One origin fact: a place, a year, a former job, a first wine.
PATH      What he did before; where he learned; one named mentor or grower.
FIRST     The first estate signed and why; the first case count or shipment.
NOW       A working fact: visits per year, harvests worked, who tastes.
VOICE     One voice per section (third person on About; first person allowed on a letter page).
```

**Verdict on the current copy.** The About page founder section has two sentences:

> "Rawlings founded Enotri Wine Imports to bring wines of genuine provenance to Alberta — bottles that carry the mark of the people and places behind them."
>
> "He works directly with the growers in the portfolio, tasting in their cellars and walking their vineyards before a single wine is listed."

They contain no founding year, no prior career, no first estate and no number. Against the benchmarks above, they read as a placeholder. The site's one hard founder fact is on the sustainability page instead ("Having worked in South African vineyards and cellars, our founder has seen firsthand…"). Move it to the founder section. Moving from "we" to "Rawlings… He" across sections is acceptable as long as each section keeps one voice.

The footer email is rallings@enotri.ca, and one gallery caption reads "Rallings of Enotri", while the founder's name is Rawlings. Confirm that the address is spelled as intended, and fix the caption.

**Ready-to-use Enotri copy.**

> Rawlings Munemo founded Enotri Wine Imports in Edmonton in [year]. Before that he [prior path], and he has worked in South African vineyards and cellars. The first estate he signed was [estate], in [year].

> He still tastes in every cellar before a wine is listed: [number] visits in [year], from [region] to [region]. Trade enquiries come to him directly, and he answers them himself.

## Portfolio pages: facts first, story second, flavour last

### Producers index: give each grower a line, not just a region

**What the best houses do.**
- Dressner's footer is a formula of count plus named countries: "Louis/Dressner Selections is a portfolio of over 100 vignerons hailing from France, Italy, Germany, Portugal, Slovenia and Chile." ([Louis/Dressner](https://www.louisdressner.com/)).
- Le Sommelier states its count plainly: "We are proud to work closely with more than 150 producers around the world" ([Le Sommelier](https://www.lesommelier.com/wine-producers)).
- SevenFifty Daily notes that the largest winery in Vom Boden's book has less than 40 acres under vine ([SevenFifty Daily](https://daily.sevenfifty.com/importer-vom-boden-finds-success-by-championing-niche-producers/)). That shows how a scale fact can do the positioning work.

The index pattern is a count, the countries, and one fact per grower.

**Word bank and things to avoid.**

| Use | Avoid (why) |
|---|---|
| growers; estates; families; vignerons | "Crafted by Growers" ("crafted" echoes "hand-crafted" on Sullivan's list) |
| [count] estates, five countries | "stewards of place" (euphemism) |
| village · appellation · country | "provenance" meaning origin (the trade hears chain of custody) |
| a one-line standfirst: a name, a number, a place | "clarity, respect, and purpose" (three-part list) |
| "with Enotri since [year]" | "authentic"; "strong character, regional identity, and value" |

**Structure.**
```
H1      The growers   (or: "[Fourteen] estates, five countries")
INTRO   Two sentences: the rule + the visit practice. Then stop.
FILTER  Country
CARD    Name / Village · Appellation · Country / Standfirst, 10–18 words: a name, a number, a place
```

**Verdict on the current copy.**
- **H1 "Crafted by Growers": replace.**
- **Intro: rewrite.** It uses "stewards of place", "provenance" (in the origin sense), "clarity, respect, and purpose" and "authentic, grower-led estates … strong character, regional identity, and excellent price-quality ratio". The og description repeats the same words.
- **Cards: add a standfirst.** They show only the name and "region · country", and the region field is coarse ("Campania", "Granada", "Pfalz"). Each producer's one-line summary already exists in the data, but it is not shown on the cards.
- **Meta description "family estates and independent farmers": adjust.** It needs the Zaccagnini ownership caveat.

It did not remove the duty to substantiate environmental claims about a business, or the duty to test claims about a product ([MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/federal-government-narrows-scope-of-the-competition-acts-anti-greenwashing-provisions-as-bill-c-15-receives-royal-assent/)).

South Africa is not on AGLC's list of equivalency partners. AGLC requires "a valid organic certificate that meets the Canadian Organic Standard for every imported organic product", plus a declaration form for each certificate ([AGLC](https://aglc.ca/liquor/liquor-agencies-and-suppliers/organic-liquor-products)).
- **Fairtrade:** the whole trade chain must be certified, and using the Mark requires a licence ([FLOCERT](https://www.flocert.net/get-certified/)).

- "Agencies are not permitted to impose a minimum order quantity per product per licensee". (Both [AGLC Liquor Agency Handbook](https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LA%20Handbook.pdf).)

## Conclusion

In Alberta's agency market, precision is the gap a competitor can fill. Garneau Block owns a category label, Trialto owns a registered three-part slogan, and most other agencies use superlatives. A site that names the grower, dates every fact and names the certifier would be the only one of its kind there. It costs nothing but care. Getting the voice right and getting the facts right turn out to be the same job. A museum-label sentence has nowhere to hide an error: a fact, a place and a name are exactly what AGLC's "accurate and verifiable" standard, CFIA's certifier rule and the Competition Act's evidence-first rule all test. The adjective layer that makes the current copy generic is also where most of its legal risk sits.

The facts that would make Enotri credible are ones no competitor can copy, and ones only the founder holds: the year he started, what he did before, the first estate he signed, the number of cellars he visited last year, and the year each relationship began. Because importers publish different hectare and certification figures for the same estates, a "Facts verified [month year]" line is not fussiness. It shows a trade reader that Enotri checked with the grower directly, which is the advocate archetype in practice.
